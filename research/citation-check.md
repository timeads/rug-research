## What changed in the database
**Refuted or unsupported (now marked):**
- *"Chinese Art Deco" and "Nichols rugs" don't exist in Chinese.* Chinese sources do use both (中国装饰艺术风格的地毯; 倪克地毯厂, 1923).
- *Hand-tufting overtook hand-knotting in India around 1995–2005.* This rested on a mistranslation: हस्तनिर्मित means handmade (including hand-tufted), not hand-knotted. The quotation wasn't found either. The date is gone from the India chapter notes and the 1970–1999 era summary.

**Corrected:**
- Ningxia: about 300 of the Palace Museum's nearly 1,000 carpets (about 30%), not over half (Xinhua, 2024).
- Tianjin: over 80% of China's carpet exports in 1912–27, not 70–80%; the factory counts are 1929 figures; the 1990s decline has more than one cause. The Chinese "quotation" was the brief's own paraphrase and is no longer shown as a quote.
- Iraq is the biggest market for Tianjin-made carpet, not for all Chinese carpet.
- Beijing merchants: 李黎轩 and 燕京地毯厂, not 李立轩 and 仰经地毯厂. The "115 times 1912" export figure wasn't found.
- DHT-200 (2004): supported by Donghua University, but the Textile Leader article and its quotation weren't found. The partner may be Jiangxi, not Shanghai, Huayuan.
- CN1030783C is a carpet patent, not a tufting-machine patent.
- Guinness 2025: the record is the largest hand-tufted carpet, not the largest handcrafted carpet.
- Bhadohi fair 2025: 46 US buyers against the usual ~55, not 80–100. The tariff was cut to 18% in February 2026.
- Japan study missions began in 1971, not the 1960s. Toli was a linoleum maker; the Teijin joint venture wasn't found.
- Genemuiden 1967: confirmed by Canon van Nederland, not the Rijksdienst. The quotation shown is now Canon's.
- Balsan 1954: the quotation is from French Wikipedia, not the heritage association.
- Goswami's handmade-carpet chapters are 12–13.
- Tai Ping's gun: the 1973 motorised gun is confirmed on the Hong Kong Heritage Project page, but a 2021 trade article credits Yeh with a pneumatic gun in 1960.

**New leads the check turned up:**
- Suminoe may have imported a used narrow tufting machine in 1952, and its 1957 broadloom machine came from the US firm Super Tufter (Ōsumi, J-Stage; check the PDF).
- Belgium: Trend Mills (US) opened a tufting subsidiary at Ronse, East Flanders, in 1964.
- 方显廷 (H. D. Fong), 《天津地毯工业》 (1929–30) is reprinted in 《方显廷文存》 (2019), so it's available in print.

**Quotations not found** (shown in the database as unverified, or no longer shown as quotations): Chengdu poke-poke shop owner; "knotters' descendant"; Jabir Ansari / Amar Ujala crossover; CUTS "₹100 tufting machine"; Kara's "screwdriver-shaped gun"; the Hindi Wikipedia WWII sentence.
