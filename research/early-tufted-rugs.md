# Research brief: early tufted rugs

*Working notes, October 2026. Every claim below comes from secondary sources or search summaries and needs checking against the holding institution's own record. The matching database entries are tagged with the theme **"Early tufting: bed rugs & needlework pile"**.*

## The working argument

Tufting did not start in Dalton in the 1890s. Americans had been pushing yarn through a ground cloth with a needle, and cutting the loops into pile, since the early 1700s. The technique moved from luxury bedcovers, to whitework spreads, to a cottage industry, and finally to a machine. A lineage to test:

## Why Winterthur is the right place

- Its **bed rugs**, the Mary Foot rug (1778) above all, are the earliest dated objects in this lineage that are held where the fellowship is based.
- Its **hooked and sewn rugs** (1800–1925) are the parallel tradition to compare against: hooking pulls loops *up* from below, while needle tufting pushes yarn *down* from above. That distinction is the technical spine of a history of tufting.
- Its **trade catalog collection** (McKinstry guide, Olson catalogs) documents how floor coverings were marketed before and during the tufting boom.
- **Hagley**, a few miles away, holds the DuPont nylon records. A single fellowship trip could cover both the pre-industrial and the synthetic ends of the story.

## Open questions to resolve

1. **Technique:** which Winterthur bed rugs have *cut* pile and which looped? Ask to examine 1960.0594 and 1964.1641, including the back.
2. **First machine:** ASME credits Moench (1928) and Dalton accounts credit Looper. Find both patent numbers on Google Patents.
3. **Cobble and Muse:** identify the joint patent (James A. or Joe Cobble?).
4. **BCF nylon:** 1957 or 1959? Settle it with the Hagley Textile Fibers records.
5. **Photo date:** the Cabin Crafts / Arnall photograph is catalogued 1962, but Arnall left office in 1947.
6. **The 2024 tufting manual** reportedly cited in Winterthur rug 1965.0685's record: find out what it is and why it is cited there.
7. **Gap, 1850–1890:** is there tufted work between the end of candlewick and Whitener's revival? Search Chronicling America and HathiTrust for "tufted spread" and "candlewick" in that period.
