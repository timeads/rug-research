# Archive search brief for Claude in Chrome

You are helping Tim Eads research the history of rugs, carpet and tufting for a Winterthur fellowship application and a history of tufting for his firm, Tuft the World. The research lives in a website, the **Rug Research database**: https://timeads.github.io/rug-research/

Your job: search the archives listed below, which the database can't search for itself. For each archive, run the searches given, open the promising results, and add the relevant items to the database. Work through the archives in the order given. At the end, report back (see "When you finish").

## Ground rules

1. **Record only what the page shows.** Copy the catalogue's own wording for the title, date, maker, materials, size and number. Never fill a gap with a guess. If something is uncertain, say so in the description (for example "date given as c. 1930 on the object page; 1929 in the database view").
2. **One record per item.** A collection, a box of papers or a single photograph can each be one record. Don't make records for search-result pages.
3. **Relevance first.** Add an item only if it bears on one of the topics below. When unsure, add it with the tag `possible` and say why in the description.
4. **Don't add duplicates.** If the database says "Already in the database", open that record and add anything new with **Edit** instead. Only choose "add it as a new record" when the page shows a different item.
5. **Be a polite visitor.** Several of these sites ask automated tools not to crawl them (Hagley, the Digital Library of Georgia). Work at reading pace, and open only the pages you need. Don't download files in bulk.
6. **Never try to get past a human-verification check** ("Verifying connection", "Just a moment…", a CAPTCHA). Stop and ask Tim to complete it, then carry on.
7. **Don't log in or pay for anything.** If a resource needs a subscription (for example Gale's trade catalogues), note it in your report and move on.
8. **Images:** link to the institution's image; never upload copies. If the image doesn't load in the database, keep the record anyway.

## How to add an item to the database

Use the database in this same browser window and profile. The records are saved in this browser, and Tim exports them afterwards.

1. Open https://timeads.github.io/rug-research/ and click **Add item** (top right).
2. In **Drop in what you found**, paste, each on its own line:
   - the item's page address (its permanent link if the page offers one);
   - the image address, if there is one (right-click the image, then **Copy image address**);
   - the catalogue text from the page: title, date, maker, place, materials, dimensions, accession or call number, credit line and description.
3. Set **This is** if it's not obvious (object, company or institution, book, article, trade catalog, photo).
4. Click **Fill in the form**. If it says the page is already in the database, follow ground rule 4.
5. Check every filled field against the page and correct it:
   - **Title:** the catalogue title.
   - **Date as written:** as the catalogue gives it.
   - **Start year / End year:** numbers only; BCE years are negative.
   - **Institution or owner:** the holding institution, not the website.
   - **Accession no., call no. or ISBN.**
   - **Source link:** the item page.
   - **Image link** and **Image credit:** the institution.
   - **Image permission:** "Not yet requested".
6. Fill in these too:
   - **Description:** a short factual summary of what the item shows, using the catalogue's words. Then a line beginning "Why it matters:" that links it to a topic below. Last, a line "Found: [archive], search "[terms]", [today's date]."
   - **Tags:** `found by Claude in Chrome`, the archive's name (for example `Hagley`), the chapter it serves (for example `for: Woven carpet`), plus `possible` if unsure.
   - **Research themes:** pick from the list that appears as you type (for example "Woven carpet", "Machine tufting & Dalton", "Hooked & handmade rugs").
   - **Status:** leave "Unverified". Tim marks records verified after review.
7. Click **Save**. The record opens; check it reads correctly.

If the drop-in box misreads something, fix the field by hand; the form is the record.

## The archives, in order

For each archive: the address, what to look for, and search terms to start with. Use your judgement to follow good leads (related items, the same collection, "more like this").

### 1. Hagley Digital Archives: https://digital.hagley.org/
DuPont's nylon and its carpet fibres, 1938–1975: advertisements, product photographs, films, trade literature.
- Searches: `nylon carpet`, `Antron`, `501 nylon`, `carpet fiber`, `tufted carpet`, `broadloom`, `BCF`, `bulked continuous filament`, `Textile Fibers carpet`, `carpet nylon advertisement`, `Magee Carpet`, `Mohasco`, `Bigelow`.
- Look especially for:
  - Acc. 1803 advertising tearsheets from 1958–66 (carpet ads before Antron);
  - Acc. 1984.259 Textile Fibers product photographs (carpet images);
  - Acc. 1970.200 product information photographs;
  - Acc. 1995.300 films and commercials ("DuPont Carpet Nylon", "The Opening Door" 1959).
- Record the Hagley item number from the address (for example `1970200_09067`).

### 2. Hagley finding aids: https://findingaids.hagley.org/
Collection guides for DuPont's Textile Fibers Department and the carpet trade.
- Searches: `Textile Fibers Department`, `carpet`, `Antron`, `nylon carpet`, `bulked continuous filament`, `Carpet Institute`, `tufting`.
- Wanted: the date DuPont introduced BCF carpet nylon (1957 or 1959), and the boxes or folders that would show it. Record each relevant collection or series as one "archive" record with its accession number.

### 3. Winterthur Library catalogue (WinterCat): https://library.winterthur.org/
Trade catalogues, pattern books and manuscripts. (Winterthur's museum and library digital collections are already searchable in the database, so skip those.)
- Searches: `carpet trade catalog`, `rug patterns`, `hooked rug patterns`, `Frost hooked rug`, `Ross rug patterns`, `Bigelow carpet`, `Brussels carpet`, `ingrain carpet`, `Wilton carpet`, `carpet samples`, `floor cloth`, `bed rug`, `candlewick`, `chenille`, `tufted bedspread`, `yarn sample book`, `Arbuthnot`.
- Wanted:
  - the *Bed Ruggs/1722–1833* (1972) checklist;
  - the Arbuthnot Brussels drafts (1803–17), and where they are now;
  - any Exeter (1757) or Axminster (1765–70) carpet images credited to Winterthur;
  - carpet and rug trade catalogues and pattern books, with call numbers.

### 4. Digital Library of Georgia: https://dlg.usg.edu/
Dalton's bedspread and carpet industry in photographs, newspapers and documents.
- Searches: `chenille bedspread`, `tufted bedspread`, `candlewick`, `Dalton bedspread`, `Bedspread Boulevard`, `Cabin Crafts`, `tufting machine`, `Catherine Evans`, `Whitfield County`, `carpet mill Dalton`, `Barwick`, `World Carpets`, `Vanishing Georgia bedspread`.
- Wanted:
  - photographs of early tufting machines;
  - roadside spread sales;
  - the date of the Cabin Crafts photograph with Gov. Ellis Arnall (catalogued 1962; Arnall was governor 1943–47).

### 5. Bandy Heritage Center: https://bandyheritagecenter.pastperfectonline.com/
Dalton State College's archive of the carpet and chenille industry.
- Searches: `bedspread`, `chenille`, `tufting machine`, `Sims`, `Cabin Crafts`, `Looper`, `Cobble`, `Barwick`, `machine`, `photograph Dalton mill`.
- Wanted:
  - accession numbers, makers and dates for chenille spreads;
  - photographs of early Dalton and Chattanooga tufting machines;
  - Sims Textile catalogues, and whether the Sims sheets at the Center are originals or facsimiles.

### 6. Chronicling America (newspapers): https://www.loc.gov/collections/chronicling-america/
US newspapers to 1963.
- Searches (try each with Georgia newspapers and nationally): `tufted bedspreads`, `chenille spreads`, `Dalton bedspreads`, `candlewick spreads`, `tufting machine`, `tufted rugs`, `hand-tufted`, `broadloom`.
- Wanted: the earliest dated mentions of each term, prices for spreads (the 25–50¢ and Sears prices), and reports of the first tufting machines. Record each useful article as an "article" record with newspaper, date and page.

### 7. HathiTrust full-text search: https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/ls
Trade journals and books, searchable inside the text.
- Searches (use phrase search): `"tufted carpet"`, `"tufting machine"`, `"broadloom"` (pre-1930), `"Bigelow" "broadloom"`, `"hand tufted"`, `"tufting gun"`, `"chenille bedspread"`, `"Super Tufter"`.
- Wanted:

- Full view is often unavailable for post-1929 works; the search still shows which page a phrase is on. Record the volume and page.

## Topics: what the history still needs

These are the open questions from each chapter of the manuscript. Anything that answers one is a priority; say which question in the record's "Why it matters" line.

**Processes:**
- Hermitage inventory numbers for the Pazyryk carpet and felts.
- A museum-dated braided rug before 1860.
- Museum or scholarly sources for rölakan and trasmatta.
- Who first made knitted carpet, and when the fusion-bonding process was invented.
- Winterthur examples of each process: yarn-sewn, shirred, hooked, braided.

**Before the machine (early America):**
- Which Winterthur bed rugs have cut rather than looped pile.
- Tufted work between the candlewick spreads and Catherine Evans (c. 1850–1890).
- The *Bed Ruggs* (1972) checklist.
- Winterthur's hooked rugs, with accession numbers and dates.

**Woven carpet:**
- Winterthur's ingrain, Brussels and Wilton pieces.
- Where the Arbuthnot Brussels drafts are held.
- The Wilton dates (1721 or 1741).
- A primary source for Bigelow's first broadloom (1877), and when "broadloom" came into use.
- The Shaw Axminster loom's maker's plate.

**Dalton bedspreads:**
- Accession numbers, makers and dates for the Dalton spreads.
- The date of the Cabin Crafts/Arnall photograph.
- Sims Textile catalogues.
- The source of the 25–50¢ and Sears prices.
- Barwick's 1950–54 sales.

**Building the machine:**
- A joint Cobble–Muse patent.
- Glenn Dalton's 1936 filing.
- The first machine to tuft broadloom width (c. 1950).
- Photographs of early Dalton and Chattanooga machines.

**Nylon:**
- The BCF date (1957 or 1959).
- DuPont carpet ads from 1958–66.
- DuPont TV commercials for carpet nylon.
- Textile Fibers product photographs of carpet.

**Tufting abroad:**
- Ōsumi's paper.
- Japan Carpet Association study-mission reports (from 1971).
- The first tufting machine in West Flanders.
- France's first tufting machine.
- Dura Tufting's founding year.

**China:**
- The first imported tufting machine (vendor, year, buyer).
- Fong's 《天津地毯工业》.
- The first hand-gun tufting in mainland factories.

**Hand tools and the gun:**
- The earliest dated motorised or air tufting gun in production use.
- Tai Ping's dates.
- Any Hofmann patent.
- A dated photograph of a hand gun in use in Dalton (1950s–60s).

**India:**
- Any dated source for when hand-tufting arrived in India.

**Programmable tufting:**
- The first computer-controlled broadloom tufting machine installed in a mill.
- Launch years for Card-Monroe Infinity and Hollow Needle, and for Cobble Colortec.
- Mills that used Tapistron's CYP.

**Hobby tufting:**
- Sales or user figures for the hobby boom.
- Who first sold hobby guns online.
- The year of Craftphilia's Kickstarter.
