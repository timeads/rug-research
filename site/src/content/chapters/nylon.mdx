On 13 July 1948 DuPont put out a press release about its own hotel in Wilmington, Delaware. Nine months earlier, it said, "a cut pile rug of burgundy spun nylon in Axminster weave" had been laid in the Brandywine Room, one of the Hotel du Pont's restaurants, and a Wilton carpet in a grey and white spiral pattern was planned for the Grill Room.[@u-mvs3g9dd3zmb0] Both were woven. This chapter follows DuPont's fibre from carpets like these to the tufting machine, and the selling that made a market for the result.

## Carothers's patents, 1937 and 1938

<Figure id="pat-us2071250a" size="margin" caption="Wallace H. Carothers, “Linear condensation polymers,” US patent 2,071,250, granted 16 February 1937." />

The fibre began as patents. From Wallace Carothers's research DuPont took out "Linear condensation polymers", granted on 16 February 1937, and "Synthetic fiber", granted in 1938.[@pat-us2071250a; @pat-us2130948a] Hagley, which holds DuPont's archives, marks the first date as the day the company was granted its nylon patent, and the standard account of how DuPont turned the polymer into a product is Hounshell and Smith's *Science and Corporate Strategy*.[@hagley-vault-1937-patent; @book-hounshell-smith]

The polymer in those patents is the one now called nylon 6,6, and the name needs that care.[@pat-us2071250a] According to the research notes, which have still to cite a primary source for it, a second nylon, nylon 6, was developed separately by Paul Schlack at IG Farben in Germany in 1938, and Allied Chemical's Caprolan, a nylon 6, later competed with DuPont's fibre in tufted carpet.[@ttw-nylon-6-schlack] "DuPont invented nylon" is true only of nylon 6,6.[@ttw-claim-dupont-nylon]

DuPont's first nylon plant, at Seaford, Delaware, dates from 1939, but carpet came much later: Hagley's guide to the Seaford collection says nylon for the carpet industry was developed there in 1958.[@hagley-seaford-nylon] The first nylon carpets in DuPont's records came before that, and they came from the loom.

## The Nylon Suite at the Hotel du Pont

<Figure id="u-mvs3jcd5g72ua" size="wide" caption="The Nylon Suite at the Hotel DuPont, Wilmington. On the floor, James Lees and Sons’ “Sculptex” carpet with an all-nylon face. Hagley dates the photograph about 1945; DuPont’s 1948 press release suggests a later date." />

DuPont photographed the hotel's "Nylon Suite", in which the bedroom draperies, the chair's upholstery, the window curtain and the carpet were all nylon. On the floor was James Lees and Sons' "Sculptex" carpet, "an all nylon face in grey-green" which, the caption adds, "does not attract moths or other insects".[@u-mvs3jcd5g72ua] Hagley dates the photographs about 1945, but DuPont's press release of July 1948 describes nylon furnishings being installed in the hotel then, so they may be later.[@u-mvs3jcd5g72ua; @u-mvs3g9dd3zmb0]

The first nylon carpet on the open market came from a mill in Auburn, New York. In a release for 12 May 1948 DuPont announced the Nye-Wait Company's "Splendante", its pile woven in contrasting heights to give "a bas-relief effect", in beige, grey and pastel green.[@u-mvs379gngi80e]

<PullQuote from="u-mvs379gngi80e" who="DuPont press release, 12 May 1948">The first carpet on the market with pile completely woven of Du Pont nylon</PullQuote>

About 1955 Nye-Wait showed almost a hundred nylon carpet samples to the press in New York, all available nationally. The rug in DuPont's photograph, shown at the head of this chapter, was woven to order in two pile heights, and the caption called nylon carpeting "paradoxically the most luxurious yet in terms of wear life and upkeep, the most economical ever made", introduced five years before.[@u-mvs37wl6gkh9w] Claims about wear and upkeep had to be tested.

## Nancy Cooch's nylon hooked rug, 1950

<Figure id="hagley-nylon-hooked-rug" size="margin" caption="Nancy du Pont Reynolds Cooch’s nylon hooked rug, 1950." />

In 1950 Nancy du Pont Reynolds Cooch made a hooked rug in nylon, 116 by 139 inches, now at Hagley. The museum links it to DuPont's wear-testing of nylon for floor coverings, and walked on, it frayed and pilled.[@hagley-nylon-hooked-rug] It is a rare handmade bridge between rug-making at home and synthetic carpet, though not the only nylon hooked rug: Hagley also holds one in nylon and cotton from 1975–1990.[@hagley-2015-10-2]

Dirt was tested too. About 1955 DuPont photographed a test strip of carpet partly treated with Ludox colloidal silica: after vacuuming, "treated areas emerge clean, while untreated portions remain dirty and dingy". A companion photograph said Ludox "is rapidly being adopted by the carpet industry as a soil retardant".[@hagley-1972341-ludox]

<Figure id="u-mvs37zoguerdx" caption="A bath mat and lid cover “tufted with DuPont nylon,” about 1955." />

Another DuPont photograph of about the same date shows nylon already tufted. A bath mat and matching lid cover were "tufted with DuPont nylon", and their maker described them as machine washable.[@u-mvs37zoguerdx] A mat is a small thing. DuPont's nylon made for carpet came in 1958.

## *Nylon: The First 25 Years*, 1963

<Figure id="u-mvs3387y549fp" size="margin" caption="Nylon: The First 25 Years, DuPont’s anniversary booklet, 1963." />

In 1963 DuPont marked twenty-five years of nylon with a booklet, *Nylon: The First 25 Years*, now at Hagley. Its chronology puts the change that mattered for carpet in 1958: "A special bulked continuous filament nylon yarn was developed for carpeting; it soon revolutionized the market for rugs and carpeting", as Hagley's machine-read text gives it.[@u-mvs3387y549fp; @event-bcf-nylon] Later histories date bulked continuous filament, or BCF, to 1957 or to 1959.[@event-bcf-nylon] Tom McAndrews, who worked in DuPont's textile fibres business, recalled in a 2018 oral history that DuPont made carpet filament from 1952 and launched "501" carpet nylon, with a special crimp, in 1958.[@hagley-oh-mcandrews]

The booklet also counted the result. "Nylon for carpets now totals over 150 million pounds a year," it said, and the continuous type had helped nylon take "over 26 per cent of market for broadloom carpeting".[@u-mvs3387y549fp, image 25] The market it was taking had changed in the meantime. Tufted carpet sales, near zero in the late 1940s, passed 100 million square yards by 1958,[@article-patton-ehnet] and tufted carpet reportedly sold wholesale at about half the price of woven.[@tufted-half-price] According to the Carpet and Rug Institute, machinery developed for chenille rugs had been widened to make larger rugs and broadloom carpet (chapters 4 and 5).[@chenille-widened]

The old weavers moved as well. Mohawk and Alexander Smith, two of the great woven-carpet firms, merged as Mohasco in 1956.[@mohasco-1956] Its 1957 annual report describes new synthetic fibres in its lines, Chromspun acetate and Acrilan acrylic, and new manufacturing techniques for its tufted and velvet fabrics, for which "patents have been received or are pending".[@u-mvs3lacgfbrui, page image 7] Mills that could make the carpet still needed shops and households that would buy it.

## A DuPont Carpet Nylon advertisement, 1960

  <Figure id="u-mvs38hqlyz4s6" caption="“A Houseful of Value … All ‘501’,” DuPont advertisement, 1965." />
</Pair>

In 1960 DuPont advertised "DuPont Carpet Nylon" in the *New York Times Magazine*.[@u-mvs38atad1qhj] By then it had commissioned studies of the people it wanted to reach. Ernest Dichter's Institute for Motivational Research, which in 1952 had proposed a psychological study of "the Sales and Advertising Problems" of the Carpet Institute, the wool carpet industry's trade body,[@u-mvs3rwdznyj3f] studied for DuPont why shoppers resisted or embraced nylon carpet.[@hagley-dichter-nylon-carpets] In November 1957 it proposed a second study, of nylon carpet's acceptance among distributors, buyers and retailers.[@u-mvs3aug7hh3lv] In January 1959 DuPont's own market researchers reported on "Retailers' Attitudes Toward Carpet Fibers and the Du Pont Airwaves Program Promoting 100% Nylon Carpeting".[@u-mvs3riubri1f6]

Hagley holds films of the network drama DuPont sponsored, and "DuPont Carpet Nylon" commercials ran in *The DuPont Show with June Allyson* on 5 October 1959, 11 January 1960 and 20 February 1961, and in "The Way Home", a *DuPont Show of the Month* of 1960 with Ronald Reagan.[@hagley-film-opening-door-1959; @u-mvs3903rv1gjh; @u-mvs393k4f607p; @hagley-film-fc319] By 1962, in *The DuPont Show of the Week*, the product was "DuPont 501 Carpet Nylon".[@u-mvs3970mvyiw9]

The print campaign ran on through the mid-1960s, from "A Houseful of Value ... All '501'" (1965) to "Those Five Tough DuPont Tests" (1966).[@u-mvs38hqlyz4s6; @u-mvs38l6olbged] One advertisement of 1966 was headed:

<PullQuote from="u-mvs38omy32ao5" who="DuPont advertisement, 1966">Don’t Let Anyone Mislead You, DuPont 501 Is Not the Name of a Fiber</PullQuote>

Designers put the carpet in rooms. At the 1962 Decoration & Design Show in New York, William Pahlmann Associates laid wall-to-wall carpet of "continuous filament nylon in white and beige" by James Lees & Sons, named "Happy Day".[@hagley-1972430-1080] Competitors answered with their own fibres and their own advertising, among them Allied Chemical's Caprolan.[@duke-adviews-caprolan] DuPont's next answer was a fibre with a new name.

## Antron on a Marathon bridge, 1977

For the 1977 New York City Marathon, DuPont laid Antron carpet on a bridge for the runners, and a film of the race is among the DuPont films at Hagley.[@hagley-film-fc249-02] Antron was ten years old by then. DuPont had introduced it in 1967 under the headline "Meet Antron: The New Carpet Fiber for Enduring Elegance",[@hagley-dpads-1803-00680] and by 1969 its advertisements called Antron "the Luxury Carpet Fiber from DuPont".[@hagley-dpads-1803-00709; @hagley-dpads-1803-00712] Sarah Archer has written about this advertising and the image of luxury it sold.[@article-archer-luxury-underfoot]

The chemistry went on behind the advertising. In 1969 a DuPont chemist, Donald Hull, invented conductive fibres to control static in nylon carpet.[@hagley-hull-papers] The du Pont name reached the floor by another route as well: a rug labelled "Winterthur Collection", 100 per cent nylon and made by Shaw Rugs under licence from the Henry Francis du Pont Winterthur Museum, has appeared at auction.[@wint-shaw-licensed-nylon]

This chapter argues that DuPont's nylon reached the floor before it had a carpet fibre or a tufted market. Its first carpets were woven, shown off in the company's hotel and tested by hand. In 1958 DuPont made a bulked continuous filament nylon for carpet, as tufted carpet sales passed 100 million square yards, and the research and advertising of the following decade addressed the retailers and households who would buy the combination.[@event-bcf-nylon; @article-patton-ehnet]
