On 14 November 2022 the *Southern Metropolis Daily* (南方都市报) ran a story headed "帆板、露营之后，年轻人又开始戳戳秀了": after paddleboarding and camping, young people turn to poke-poke.[@ner-cn-pokepoke] 戳戳秀, "poke-poke", was the name under which tufting with a hand gun had become a craze in mainland China.[@ner-cn-pokepoke] The tool at its centre, the powered hand gun, had spent decades in workshops and mills (chapter 9). This chapter follows it into homes after 2018, across the world on social media, and into the hands of artists.

## A store and a manual, Philadelphia, 2018–2024

Tuft the World, the author's firm, was founded in 2018 by Tiernan Alexander and Tim Eads, artists who live and work in West Philadelphia. It sells tufting guns, tools and materials and runs workshops.[@co-tuft-the-world] Several of its store pages address beginners as well as advanced tufters.[@tool-ttw-electric-guns] The guns themselves, and who makes them, are described in chapter 9.

<Figure id="book-tuft-the-world-2024" caption="Tuft the World: An Illustrated Manual to Tufting Gorgeous Rugs, Decor, and More, 2024 (published 16 April 2024)" />

Instruction went with the tools. In March 2024 the firm gave a lecture and workshop at The Fabric Workshop and Museum,[@co-tuft-the-world] and the next month Chronicle Books published the founders' illustrated manual for tufting with a hand gun, *Tuft the World: An Illustrated Manual to Tufting Gorgeous Rugs, Decor, and More*.[@book-tuft-the-world-2024] How many people took up the hobby is not yet known: no sales or user figures have been found. Its spread is easier to see in the Chinese press.

## Poke-poke, from Hangzhou to Jinan, 2021–2022

The craze, also written 戳戳绣, moved from city to city. According to the 2022 press, tufting studios opened first in Hangzhou, then in Guangzhou, where one shop opened in September 2021, and by early 2022 in Hefei and Jinan. The press, so far read only in search-result summaries, credits overseas social media (Instagram, also TikTok and Xiaohongshu) and the artist Tim Eads, with the year 2018, as sparks of the trend.[@ner-cn-pokepoke] Eads is the author of this book, and the attribution is the press's, not a measured influence.[@co-tuft-the-world]

Social media carried studios as well as hobbyists. An Instagram tufting video in 2022 brought the Swiss studio Kramis, founded in 1987, international attention (chapter 11).[@co-kramis] It also carried the work of artists who took the gun much further.

## Artists at the gun, 2018 onwards

The gun that came home also found artists who took it far beyond the hobby. Trish Andersen grew up in Dalton, where her grandfather had moved from Chicago in 1949 to sell latex to the carpet industry, and she studied fibers at the Savannah College of Art and Design.[@artist-trish-andersen-press] In 2018 she tufted a stair runner made to look like paint running down the stairs, and the response to it on Instagram, where she is @[trishandersenart](https://www.instagram.com/trishandersenart/), turned her to rugs; in 2021 she launched a collection that grew from it.[@artist-trish-andersen-press] Her studio describes work that combines "fibers gathered from field, sheep, and those developed on a factory floor".[@u-mvvp1gk2clqms] By the author's own account, she was already tufting when he began, and the two became friends at a distance and traded what they learned.[@finding-ttw-artist-relations]

Others pushed the pile into relief. David Nott, in Boston, tufts abstract designs and then carves the pile with scissors and razors into raised, sculpted pieces; he turned to textile art in 2021, by one profile, and is @[davidnott](https://www.instagram.com/davidnott/) on Instagram.[@artist-david-nott] A rainbow-road piece commissioned by Sphere Las Vegas for Dead & Company took 107 hours, according to My Modern Met.[@artist-david-nott] In Portland, Oregon, the self-taught artist Melissa Monroe began with rugs and moved on to masks and furniture: *Candy Scoop* (2024) is a vintage chair reupholstered in hand-tufted wool, and *Double Trouble* (2025) a double-headed alligator bench. She is @[melissamonroeart](https://www.instagram.com/melissamonroeart/) on Instagram.[@artist-melissa-monroe] In San Diego, Venus Perez sculpts loop pile by trimming it to cut pile at different heights, working with punch needles and a tufting gun; she is @[fun.n.rec](https://www.instagram.com/fun.n.rec/) on Instagram.[@artist-venus-perez]

Some took the gun to very large audiences. SIMJI, a textile artist and content creator born in South Korea and raised in Los Angeles, shares her tufting videos with millions of viewers, by her own and her publisher's account, as @[simjiofficial](https://www.instagram.com/simjiofficial/), and in November 2024 published *Rug Tufting with SIMJI*, a manual for beginners.[@u-mvvp4rctc7w6k; @book-rug-tufting-with-simji-2024] Matty Benedetto's Unnecessary Inventions, a Vermont studio of satirical products, posted a tutorial on making rugs with a tufting gun and has sold rugs among its inventions; it is @[unnecessaryinventions](https://www.instagram.com/unnecessaryinventions/) on Instagram.[@artist-unnecessary-inventions] By the author's account, it uses Tuft the World's supplies and is making a line of pieces based on the Pantone colour book.[@finding-ttw-artist-relations] Madeline Ronzoni founded Happy Rugs in the summer of 2020, before her final year studying marketing at Bentley University; her TikTok following has been put at nearly half a million and, in another account, over a million, and in May 2025 Rugs.com launched her first nationwide collection. She is @[happy.rugs](https://www.instagram.com/happy.rugs/) on Instagram.[@artist-madeline-ronzoni]

<Figure id="artist-unnecessary-inventions" caption="Unnecessary Inventions (Matty Benedetto): tufting gun rugs and a Pantone rug, Vermont, 2020s" />

Tufting has also reached galleries and museum collections. Qualeasha Wood, @[qualeasha](https://www.instagram.com/qualeasha/), makes tufted and jacquard-woven tapestries that join textile craft to the imagery of digital life. Her 2023 exhibition "TL;DR" at Pippy Houldsworth Gallery in London included tufted works in wool and acrylic such as *Into the Blue*, a figure in a bath among pink tiles and rubber ducks, and the Metropolitan Museum has acquired her woven tapestry *The [Black] Madonna/Whore Complex* (2021).[@artist-qualeasha-wood] Tuft the World, the author's firm, has collaborated with her on some work, by his account.[@finding-ttw-artist-relations] In March 2023 the firm held TuftCon '23, Philadelphia's first tufting convention, with workshops and lectures and an exhibition of tufted work, Pile Paragons, curated by Wood; it included a tufted portrait of Frida Kahlo by Venus Perez.[@event-tuftcon-2023] These eight artists are a handful among many.

This chapter argues that what changed after 2018 was where the powered hand gun was used. Long a tool of workshops and mills (chapter 9), it became a home pastime, sold with instruction by supply firms such as the author's own; carried by social media, according to the Chinese press, it reached mainland China as poke-poke by 2021; and artists took it into carved and sculptural work, into books and videos for beginners, and into galleries.
