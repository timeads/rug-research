In 1930 a donor to the Auckland Museum picked up a rug-hooking tool in Eden Terrace, Auckland. It has wooden handles painted with flowers on black, a large needle in the centre, and a maker's mark in gold on a handle: "Jewel Pat. Allowed". The museum calls it a semi-automated tool for punching pile through backing, patented in the United States in 1886.[@wc-83070426] Hand tools for punching pile into a backing are much older than the tufting hobby that took off after 2018. This chapter follows such tools through boxed kits to the powered gun, and to what changed when the gun came home.

<Figure id="wc-83070426" size="margin" caption="“Jewel” rug-hooking tool, patented in the United States in 1886, picked up in Auckland in 1930." />

## Ye “Susan Burr”, in its box from Torrington

The "Susan Burr" was a boxed hand rug machine with printed instructions, of a type dated to the 1920s. The box front reads "Made by The Holley Associates, Torrington, Conn."[@ttw-susan-burr] Inside are two wooden handles that slide against each other, a steel needle in one and a gauge in the other. The instructions tell the user to "push down the gauge to hold the loop while the needle is withdrawn", alternately inserting the needle and the gauge.[@ttw-susan-burr] The research notes on the box find that its mechanism copies Ebenezer Ross's patent of 1891 (chapter 9), and that no record of a real Susan Burr exists.[@ttw-susan-burr] This box, and the Ronco kit below, are in the collection of Tuft the World, the firm co-founded by this book's author.[@ttw-susan-burr; @ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit]

<Figure id="ttw-susan-burr" caption="Box front of Ye “Susan Burr” Hooked Rug Machine, with its poem and “Made by The Holley Associates, Torrington, Conn.”" />

The box gave the tool a past. A poem on it ends:[@ttw-susan-burr]

<PullQuote from="ttw-susan-burr" who="Poem on the box of Ye “Susan Burr” Hooked Rug Machine">Susan Burr, in the days gone by, / Fashioned rugs such as you or I / With the self-same needle / In the self-same way.</PullQuote>

The same two-handed action, needle and then holder in turn, was on sale again half a century later.

## Ronco's Speed-Tufting Kit, 1975

<Figure id="ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit" image={1} caption="Contents of the Ronco kit: the moulded-plastic tufting tool, fabric shredder, yarn, rug needle, latex sample, printed burlap backing and pattern sheet." />

In 1975 Ronco sold a "Speed-Tufting Kit" on television for $9.95. The box end says it was distributed exclusively by Ronco Teleproducts of Chicago, and the burlap backing inside is printed with an owl and "© 1975 Southbury Mfg. Corp.", the Connecticut maker.[@ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit] The tool is moulded plastic, with a needle handle and a looper handle worked in turn. The booklet explains: "With the Looper all the way up punch the needle through the backing material to form the first loop. Then push the Looper down to hold down the formed loop."[@ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit]

The kit was built for home use from end to end. A fabric shredder cut old clothes, sheets or blankets into strips to tuft with, and an order sheet sold frames, latex and patterns by mail.[@ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit] The patent very likely behind it, filed by John S. Doyel in October 1975, was titled "Rug-tufting device suitable for home use by hobyists".[@pat-us3968757a; @ttw-ronco-speed-tufting-kit]

Tools with the same two-part alternating action are still sold new in Mirzapur, where Naksh Enterprises offers one for cut and loop pile up to 45 mm, at around ₹1,500 (chapter 10).[@ttw-mirzapur-manual] All of these were manual tools. The powered gun has a separate history, in workshops and mills.

## The ZQ-III, from Shantou

The ZQ-III is a pneumatic tufting gun from Dayang, a carpet-machinery maker in Shantou, Guangdong. Dayang's website, seen so far only in search excerpts, calls it "a new portable type tufting machine" that combines an "electric & pneumatic device", and lists an electric tufting gun among its large tufting machines.[@tool-dayang-guns]

Guns like it have a history told in chapter 9. According to the Hong Kong Heritage Project, Tai Ping fitted the tufting gun with a motor and reduced its weight in 1973, though other sources give other dates.[@ner-hk-taiping-1973] The other records gathered for this book find the gun in workshops and mills rather than homes. Kramis in Switzerland makes bespoke rugs with single-needle tufting guns, and Shaw's design team told Eads in conversation that hand guns still mend broadloom at final inspection.[@co-kramis; @finding-shaw-mending-gun] The German firm Hofmann, whose beginnings chapter 9 follows, still lists a pneumatic hand-tufting gun for cut and loop pile, the VML 16, according to its trade-fair listing as seen in search results.[@tool-hofmann-vml16] In Meerut, Sheru Bros Engineering Works, established in 1965 according to its own listing (another directory says fourteen years), advertises electric hand tufting guns on IndiaMART, one at about ₹9,000, and says it exports them to Egypt and South Africa. Every one of those details is the seller's own.[@ner-in-sheru]

Tuft the World, the author's firm, created a store record for the ZQ-III in May 2018, though that is not a confirmed first-sale date. Its listing says the gun is "used in factory settings" and does both cut and loop pile.[@tool-ttw-pneumatic-guns] By the author's own account, Tuft the World's ZQ guns were made by Dayang.[@finding-ttw-gun-suppliers]

## Electric guns from a Philadelphia store, 2018–2024

<Figure id="tool-ttw-electric-guns" size="margin" caption="Electric hand tufting guns sold by Tuft the World." />

Tuft the World was founded in 2018 by Tiernan Alexander and Tim Eads, artists who live and work in West Philadelphia. It sells tufting tools and materials and runs workshops.[@co-tuft-the-world] Its electric guns are sold under its own name, and several of the store pages address beginners as well as advanced tufters. By the dates of its own store records the first two, the AK-I for cut pile and the AK-II for loop pile, were listed in 2018, followed by the KRD-I, which switches between cut and loop, and then the Duo and the AK5, which do both. Most weigh about 3 lb.[@tool-ttw-electric-guns] For high shag pile it has sold pneumatic guns, the ZQ-III and the AK-III, which need an air compressor as well as electricity.[@tool-ttw-pneumatic-guns] By the author's own account, the guns other than the ZQ series are supplied under Tuft the World's name.[@finding-ttw-gun-suppliers]

Instruction followed the tools. In March 2024 the firm gave a lecture and workshop at The Fabric Workshop and Museum,[@co-tuft-the-world] and the next month Chronicle Books published the founders' illustrated manual for tufting with a hand gun, *Tuft the World: An Illustrated Manual to Tufting Gorgeous Rugs, Decor, and More*.[@book-tuft-the-world-2024] How many people took up the hobby is not yet known: no sales or user figures have been found. Its spread is easier to see in the Chinese press.

## The Southern Metropolis Daily, 14 November 2022

On 14 November 2022 the *Southern Metropolis Daily* (南方都市报) ran a story headed "帆板、露营之后，年轻人又开始戳戳秀了": after paddleboarding and camping, young people turn to poke-poke.[@ner-cn-pokepoke] 戳戳秀, also written 戳戳绣, "poke-poke", was the name under which tufting with a hand gun became a craze in mainland China.[@ner-cn-pokepoke]

According to the 2022 press, tufting studios opened first in Hangzhou, then in Guangzhou, where one shop opened in September 2021, and by early 2022 in Hefei and Jinan. The press, so far read only in search-result summaries, credits overseas social media (Instagram, also TikTok and Xiaohongshu) and the artist Tim Eads, with the year 2018, as sparks of the trend.[@ner-cn-pokepoke] Eads is the author of this book, and the attribution is the press's, not a measured influence.[@co-tuft-the-world]

Social media carried studios as well as hobbyists. An Instagram tufting video in 2022 brought the Swiss studio Kramis, founded in 1987, international attention (chapter 11).[@co-kramis] While the gun was going into homes, it was also being fitted back onto machines.

## A gun for a carpet robot, Shandong, 2020

<Figure id="pat-cn111705448a" size="margin" caption="Yangxin Ruixin Group, CN 111705448 A, a pneumatic tufting gun for a carpet robot, published 25 September 2020." />

In June 2020 the Yangxin Ruixin Group, a Shandong carpet company, filed for a "pneumatic multifunctional carpet tufting gun for an intelligent gun-tufted carpet robot", published that September.[@pat-cn111705448a] The gun is meant to be the working end of a robot.[@pat-cn111705448a] It is one of a cluster of Chinese filings for automated gun-tufting: a yarn-feeding and tensioning device for a gun-tufting machine, filed in January 2019, and a "fully automatic carpet gun-tufting machine head", published in November 2020, both known so far only from search summaries.[@pat-cn111441143a; @pat-cn111876919a]

In 2023 Tuft the World showed editioned rugs made on a large robotic tufting machine at Paradigm Gallery in Philadelphia.[@event-ttw-editions-2023] Chapter 12 follows the machines.

This chapter argues that home tufting did not begin with the gun. Hand tools worked by needle and holder in turn were sold boxed in the 1920s and on television, for home use, in 1975. What changed after 2018 was that the powered hand gun, which the other records gathered here place in workshops and mills, became a home pastime, carried by social media according to the Chinese press, and reached mainland China as poke-poke by 2021, while in the same years the gun was being mounted on machines again.
