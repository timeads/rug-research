A photograph catalogued by the Bandy Heritage Center in Dalton is described as showing a Cobble Brothers loop-pile tufting machine 216 inches wide, fitted with a scroll-type pattern attachment, with an 18-inch pattern repeat. It carries no date.[@finding-cnc-mechanical-pattern-attachments] On a broadloom machine the pattern came from how much yarn each needle got, and sets of yarn-feed rolls in the attachment metered it.[@finding-cnc-mechanical-pattern-attachments] This chapter follows who, or what, decided that amount, from chains to computers, and a second line in which the hand tufting gun was put on a computer-guided frame. The multi-needle machine itself is the subject of chapter 5.

## Joseph Card's patent drawings, 1955 and 1956

<Figure id="pat-us2935037a" size="margin" caption="Joseph L. Card, “Apparatus for Making Patterned Tufted Pile Fabric”, filed 24 December 1956 for Cobble Brothers Machinery Co.: sheet 1." />

Joseph L. Card drew mechanisms of this kind. He filed for a "Thread Feed Mechanism and Pattern Control" on 16 May 1955,[@pat-us2862465a] and on 24 December 1956 for an "Apparatus for Making Patterned Tufted Pile Fabric", assigned to Cobble Brothers Machinery Co. of Chattanooga and granted in 1960.[@pat-us2935037a]

Such devices had a fixed limit, which Tuftco's patents of 1996–97 describe in looking back. The yarn-feed rolls were driven by chains or other linkage from the main drive shaft, so a whole pattern could use only two or three stitch heights, in fixed ratios to the shaft.[@finding-cnc-mechanical-pattern-attachments; @pat-us6244203b1] No dated first scroll attachment has been found.[@finding-cnc-mechanical-pattern-attachments]

Singer Cobble was already looking beyond the chain in 1961. Its earliest electrical pattern-control patent found so far, the "Photocell controlled pattern attachment for tufting machines", was filed on 29 May 1961: a photocell, not a cam or chain, controls the pattern attachment. Later patents cite it as prior art, among them a German "Elektrische Steuervorrichtung für eine Tuftingmaschine". How the photocell read the pattern is still to be confirmed from the patent's text.[@pat-us3103187a] By the mid-1960s carpet mills were patenting yarn-feed pattern control too: Callaway Mills filed a "Pattern attachment" on 18 April 1967.[@pat-us3375797a] One early use of pattern data in a carpet mill went to the yarn instead of the machine.

## Westpoint Pepperell's colour stations, 1976

<Figure id="pat-us4106416a" size="margin" caption="Westpoint Pepperell, “Control apparatus for textile dyeing and tufting machinery”, published 15 August 1978: sheet 1." />

A Westpoint Pepperell patent, "Control apparatus for textile dyeing and tufting machinery", with a priority date of 2 December 1976, puts the data at the dye stations instead of the needles. Each yarn is dyed at several colour stations whose yarn-control assemblies are "individually controllable by digital color pattern data", and the yarns are then fed to a tufting station to make a predetermined complex design.[@pat-us4106416a]

In this patent the design existed as data before a single tuft was made, and the tufting machine received yarn that already carried it. A patent filed twelve years later put a computer in charge of the machine itself.

## US patent 4,867,080, granted 1989

<Figure id="pat-us4867080a" caption="Taylor, Neely and Card, “Computer controlled tufting machine”, US patent 4,867,080, granted 19 September 1989: sheet 1." />

Brooks E. Taylor, Marshall A. Neely and Roy T. Card filed in December 1988 for a "Computer controlled tufting machine and a process of controlling the parameters of operation of a tufting machine", granted on 19 September 1989. In it a computer controls motors on the backing feed and the yarn feed, so that stitch rate, pile height and face-yarn weight are changed from the computer instead of by changing sheaves on the gearbox.[@pat-us4867080a]

Later patents cite it as the start of computer control giving "greater precision and variety" in patterned tufting, though no source found calls it the first computer-controlled tufting machine.[@pat-us4867080a] Card-Monroe's timeline lists Command Performance under 1989; a link with this patent has not been shown.[@co-card-monroe; @pat-us4867080a] Card and Taylor followed with a Card-Monroe patent for "enhanced graphic appearances" in tufted carpet, filed in 1989, which a patent of 2003 credits with computerised design centres, where designers develop patterns on a computer.[@pat-us5058518a]

The chain itself went in Tuftco's patents of the late 1990s. Its "Independent servo motor controlled scroll-type pattern attachment for tufting machine and computerized design system", with a provisional application of 27 November 1996, gives each set of yarn-feed rolls its own servo motor, and a computerised design system to handle "the large numbers of individually controllable design parameters".[@pat-us6244203b1] Servo-driven rolls could set how much yarn each needle took. Which yarn it took was a separate problem, and another line of machines had already set out to solve it.

## The CYP machine from Ringgold, Georgia

<Figure id="pat-us5165352a" size="margin" caption="Gary L. Ingram, “Hollow needle tufting apparatus for producing patterned fabric”, US patent 5,165,352, granted 24 November 1992 and assigned to Tapistron International." />

Gary L. Ingram's "Hollow needle tufting apparatus for producing patterned fabric", filed on 27 December 1991 and assigned to Tapistron International, places colour by moving the cloth. Reciprocating hollow needles carry the yarns, and the backing is shifted so that each colour lands where the pattern needs it.[@pat-us5165352a]

Ingram had started from an older idea. Kile's US 4,549,496, issued on 29 October 1985, is described as a multi-head machine with pneumatically fed hollow needles placing yarns of different colours. Ingram's "Tufting apparatus", dated 12 February 1991, is described as a modification of it that shifts the backing sideways instead of the needle heads, so that much less weight moves.[@pat-us4991523a]

Tapistron, of Ringgold, Georgia, was founded in 1986 according to Bloomberg, and made the machine as the CYP, for Computerized Yarn Placement. A FloorDaily article dates the CYP to 1992, later than Ingram's first patents, and describes it as slow but able to tuft woven-looking patterns in six component colours. In July 2001 Chattanoogan.com reported that Tapistron had filed for bankruptcy in 1996, had sold equipment to Shaw Industries, and was being sold at auction.[@co-tapistron] The broadloom builders took up colour placement in the 2000s.

## ColorPoint at the FloorTek Expo, Dalton, 2009

Card-Monroe filed its "Yarn color placement system" on 15 February 2008: yarns of different colours are coordinated so that only the selected colour shows at each stitch.[@pat-us8141505b2] A preview of the FloorTek Expo in Dalton in 2009 announced the result. Card-Monroe was introducing ColorPoint, which places "up to six colors or more anywhere in a carpet pattern with pinpoint accuracy", and Tuftco had introduced its own ColorTron earlier that year.[@event-colorpoint-colortron-2009]

The two firms met in court. In 2014 Card-Monroe sued Tuftco over its ColorTuft and iTuft machines. The parties settled on 6 November 2017, and an agreed order held the patents valid and enforceable and enjoined Tuftco from selling single-needle-bar ColorTuft and iTuft c machines.[@event-cmc-v-tuftco-2017]

In Europe, Cobble, part of the Belgian Vandewiele group since 2013 according to Vandewiele's timeline,[@co-cobble] built Colortec, a graphics machine whose needles "tuft only where pile is required". It existed by 2018, and at ITMA 2019 Cobble launched the Colortec L+, with individually controlled needles.[@event-cobble-colortec-icn] In August 2023 Card-Monroe turned to the hollow needle, filing for a hollow-needle machine for patterns in several yarn colours. Its filings say that earlier hollow-needle machines, of the Kile, Davis and Tapistron line, were limited by needle spacing in how many colours each needle could take.[@pat-us12180632b2] All of this happened inside the broadloom machine. The second line began with a single needle.

## Wilcom's tufting head, Sydney

On this second line the earliest patent found came from a maker of embroidery software. Wilcom, a Sydney maker of CAD/CAM software for embroidery, carpets and rugs,[@co-wilcom-autotuft] filed in 1994 for a "Method and system of tufting". A tufting head is moved automatically over a backing stretched on a frame, and the needle position is corrected for the direction of traverse, the yarn and the backing.[@pat-us5503092a]

<Pair>

  <Figure id="pat-cn104562491a" caption="Shenyang Institute of Automation and Yiwu New Rainbow Craft Carpet Co., “Fully computerised servo 3D gun-tufted carpet machine”, filed 19 December 2014: sheet 1." />
</Pair>

Wilcom's "Tufting machine", with an Australian priority date of 23 December 2004, moves a single hollow-needle head from vector design files, with a cutter that switches between cut and loop pile. Its background notes that tufting guns "may be controlled and guided manually by a human operator" (chapter 9).[@pat-ep1674605a1] A second patent, with priority of 4 January 2008, added varied pile height, and its background describes the hand guns:[@pat-ep2077348b1]

<PullQuote from="pat-ep2077348b1" who="Wilcom Pty Ltd, “An improved tufting machine”, priority 2008">a traditional hand tufting gun for instance, Hofmann™ or Hartleb™, each of which are limited to tufting along an outline at a constant pile height</PullQuote>

Wilcom sold the machines as AutoTuft. In 2012 Textile World reported a pneumatic head with a rotating needle for full-speed cornering and cut/loop switching tuft by tuft, and new agents in India and China, for machines making custom rugs with a "hand-tufted" look for less than hand tufting.[@co-wilcom-autotuft]

In China one such machine came from a research institute working with a carpet maker. On 19 December 2014 the Shenyang Institute of Automation of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and a Yiwu carpet maker, the Yiwu New Rainbow Craft Carpet Co., with others, applied for a "Fully computerised servo 3D gun-tufted carpet machine": a tufting head on a servo-driven X–Y mechanism, with a cutter so that cut and loop pile can be switched freely.[@pat-cn104562491a]

In May 2025 China Daily reported that Yangxin Ruixin Group and Donghua University had produced "China's first fully digital intelligent robot for hand-tufted carpets", which follows pre-programmed designs; the "first" is the newspaper's claim, and the article gives no date.[@event-yangxin-donghua-robot] Yangxin Ruixin filed in 2020 for a pneumatic gun for an intelligent gun-tufted carpet robot,[@pat-cn111705448a] one of several Chinese filings on automated gun-tufting machines that search results date to 2019 and 2020.[@pat-cn111441143a; @pat-cn111876919a] In the same years the gun on a machine reached the individual maker.

## Owen Trueblood's gun on a robot arm, 2022

<Figure id="event-trueblood-robotic-tufting-2022" size="margin" caption="From Owen Trueblood's robotic tufting project on Hackaday.io, 2022." />

In February 2022 Owen Trueblood posted a log on Hackaday.io showing how he had taken a consumer tufting gun and replaced its motor controller with an Arduino. He added a jack carrying an on/off input and a speed input, so that a robot could drive the gun. Hackaday covered it in March as the slow kind of machine that can tuft any pattern, unlike fast industrial tufters made for fixed ones.[@event-trueblood-robotic-tufting-2022] According to a write-up of the collaboration, the gun went on a UR5 robot arm, and for the Ground Truth IRL exhibition in April 2023 it tufted the shapes of New York park wildlife zones, filled in by hand with a punch needle.[@event-trueblood-robotic-tufting-2022]

Trueblood's was a hacked consumer gun; firms had brought tufting robots into custom rug making earlier (chapter 11). In 2013 Delos, of Calhoun, Georgia, launched an aircraft-carpet division using a custom-made robotic tufting machine. Reporting its 2016 lookbook, a trade paper said the robots sped up the work but about 85% of it was still done by craftsmen.[@finding-cnc-delos-robotic-2013] In 2017 Carpet Sign, of Asten in the Netherlands, acquired a 3-D tufting robot able to vary pile height without stopping, and had Karim Rashid design a collection for it, according to Interior Design.[@co-carpet-sign] In Milan, cc-tapis says it uses "automated robotufting technology" for one-off pieces, while most of its collections are still made by hand in Nepal and India.[@finding-cnc-european-robotufting-studios]

Tuft the World, the Philadelphia firm co-founded by this book's author,[@co-tuft-the-world] showed rugs made on a large robotic tufting machine at Paradigm Gallery in 2023.[@event-ttw-editions-2023] Reporting on the firm's CNC machine in 2024, Technical.ly noted that its users still adjust tuft density and row spacing by hand and change yarn colours and tuft heads, and that makers disagree about what counts as handmade; the firm lists its pieces as "robo-tufted and hand-finished in Philadelphia".[@finding-cnc-ttw-cnc-machine] Chapter 13 follows the hand gun's return to the home in the same years.

This chapter argues that programmable tufting grew along two separate lines. On the broadloom machine, control of the pattern passed from chain-driven yarn-feed rolls to computers and servo motors, and then to the choice of colour at each needle. The single hand gun, from the 1990s, was carried over the backing by a computer-guided frame, and in the studios that now use such robots, people still adjust the machine and finish the rugs by hand.
