In October 2025 *Aaj Tak* reported that a carpet from Bhadohi, made for the Grand Mosque in Astana, Kazakhstan, had entered Guinness World Records as the largest hand-tufted carpet. It measured about 12,464 square metres, took about 1,000 artisans, and was made by Hands Carpets / Patodia Contract.[@ner-in-guinness-2025] The record itself has not yet been found on the Guinness site.[@ner-in-guinness-2025] India's hand-tufting is visible at that scale. When it began is not.

## A colonial carpet belt

Europeans founded some of the best-known firms in the carpet belt of Uttar Pradesh that hand-tufting later joined. Hands says it descends from a carpet business that A. Tellery, a European, set up in 1881 to organise Bhadohi's weavers, and that after Independence the business was sold to a Calcutta Marwari house; the Patodia family took control around 1980 (another account says at the end of the Raj) and built the Hands brand. The continuity from Tellery to Hands is the company's own account.[@co-hands; @ner-in-colonial-bhadohi]

Obeetee, at Mirzapur, takes its name from the initials of its three British founders, F. H. Oakley, F. H. Bowden and J. A. L. Taylor, who set it up in 1920; the partnership was dissolved in 1932 and replaced by a company named Obeetee.[@co-obeetee] Both firms now make hand-tufted carpet alongside hand-knotted. Obeetee describes the process as yarn punched into canvas with a tufting gun and then glued to a backing, but when it began tufting has not been found.[@co-obeetee; @co-hands]

## No date for the gun

No source yet found dates the arrival of hand-tufting in India.[@ner-in-date-gap] The research brief reports that a Shodhganga thesis, papers from IICT, the carpet-technology institute at Bhadohi, the Carpet Export Promotion Council's history, the National Handicrafts Policy report, the Harvard FXB Center's report *Tainted Carpets* and a CUTS International case study were all checked and are silent on the date, though the full texts could not all be re-read.[@ner-in-date-gap]

The nearest thing to a date is one sentence, without a reference, in a 2019 article on India's carpet-weaving tradition by Pranshi Jain, an assistant professor of architecture and planning at MITS Gwalior, who lists tufted carpets among "contemporary" carpets.[@ner-in-jain]

<PullQuote from="ner-in-jain" who="Pranshi Jain, “Carpet Weaving Tradition of India”, 2019">In India, this technique came after the India achieved independence. This was the innovation technique which was introduced in this industry for making the production faster.</PullQuote>

When hand-tufting overtook hand-knotting is also undated. An earlier reading put the change between 1995 and 2005. It rested on a Hindi passage, since not found again, about the shrinking market share of हस्तनिर्मित carpet, read as hand-knotted; the word means handmade, which in the trade includes hand-tufted, so the passage says nothing about tufting against knotting.[@ner-in-crossover] *Amar Ujala*'s reports of 2025 and 2026 say only that knotted carpets are now a token share of exports and that hand-tufted and handloom goods dominate.[@ner-in-crossover]

## Teaching and making the tools

The tools are taught. A syllabus for the B.Tech in carpet and textile technology, issued by U.P. Technical University and hosted by IICT Bhadohi as its old syllabus, includes the "practice of tufting using hand & electric gun" and lists "tufting equipments used e.g. (tufting frame, tufting gun etc.) Schortz hand tufted machine".[@ner-in-iict-syllabus] IICT runs under the Ministry of Textiles.[@ner-in-iict-syllabus] K. K. Goswami, named as IICT's director, edited *Advances in Carpet Manufacture* (second edition 2017), which has chapters on handmade carpets and a separate chapter on tufting.[@ner-chase-goswami] His publisher's biography calls him the "inventor of a new form of carpet backing"; no Indian patent for a tufting gun has been found.[@ner-in-backing-patents]

The tools are made and sold locally. In Mirzapur, Naksh Enterprises sells new manual speed-tufting tools for cut and loop pile up to 45 mm, at around ₹1,500, though commercial pile work uses 220-volt electric guns.[@ttw-mirzapur-manual] The manual tool's American history is told in chapter 9. At Meerut, Sheru Bros Engineering Works, a partnership firm that lists its founding as 1965, advertises electric hand-tufting guns, one at ₹9,000, and says it exports them to Egypt and South Africa; all of this is the seller's own listing.[@ner-in-sheru]

## Borrowed words

Hindi has a word for tufted carpet, गुच्छित कालीन (*guchchhit kālīn*), from गुच्छ, a tuft or cluster. It appears in the Hindi Wikipedia article on carpets, and a search for the exact phrase found it on no other Hindi page.[@ner-in-vocabulary] The trade writes English words in Devanagari instead: हैंड टफ्टेड (hand tufted) and टफ्टिंग गन (tufting gun), as in an *Amar Ujala* headline on हैंड टफ्टेड कालीनों.[@ner-in-vocabulary] The research also recorded a sentence from the same Wikipedia article saying that the machine for making carpet by stitching was invented in the southern United States shortly after the Second World War. It did not appear when the page was searched again, and its old-fashioned phrasing suggests it was taken from a mid-century Hindi encyclopedia.[@ner-in-hindi-wiki]

## Exporters, Panipat and the machine

The exporters span knotting and tufting. Surya began in 1976 in Ugapur, in the Bhadohi area, as a small supplier of hand-knotted rugs, and is now a large American supplier of hand-knotted, hand-tufted and flatwoven rugs.[@co-surya] Jaipur Living, founded in Rajasthan in 1978, is mainly a hand-knotting firm, but also makes rugs gun-tufted through a pre-woven cloth with a cotton-latex backing.[@co-jaipur] Kaleen India, begun in 1996, has plants in Panipat, Mirzapur and Bikaner making hand-knotted, hand-tufted, flatweave and power-loom rugs.[@co-kaleen-india] These firms, and their American offices, return in chapter 11.

Panipat, in Haryana, became a carpet town by migration. A CUTS International case study says the industry spread there "where the weavers migrated from Bhadohi-Mirzapur".[@ner-in-panipat-migration] The town is now also a centre of textile recycling: *The Tribune* reports more than 30 lakh kilograms of recycled yarn a day, and about 200 spinning mills (150 in another version of the story).[@ner-in-panipat-recycling]

Industrial carpet came separately. Welspun Flooring was formed in March 2016 as a subsidiary of the home-textile maker Welspun India, and its plant near Hyderabad, under construction in 2018 for a start in 2019, makes carpet tiles, broadloom, artificial grass and click vinyl. The company calls it Asia's largest; when it was commissioned has not been confirmed.[@co-welspun-flooring]

The export trade is exposed. A 50 per cent American tariff took effect on 27 August 2025. *Amar Ujala* reported that 46 American buyers were expected at Bhadohi's October fair, against a usual 55 or so, and the fair had about 110 fewer stalls. The tariff was cut to 18 per cent in an India–US agreement in February 2026.[@ner-in-tariff-2025]

This chapter argues that hand-tufting entered an export belt whose oldest firms were founded by Europeans, left no dated record of its arrival, and is still named in the trade in borrowed English words. Its weight in the trade today is reported; its beginning remains to be found.
