In October 2025 *Aaj Tak* reported that a carpet from Bhadohi had entered Guinness World Records as the largest hand-tufted carpet. Made by Hands Carpets / Patodia Contract for the Grand Mosque in Astana, Kazakhstan, it measured about 12,464 square metres and took about 1,000 artisans.[@ner-in-guinness-2025] The record itself has not yet been found on the Guinness site.[@ner-in-guinness-2025] India's hand-tufting can be seen at that scale. When it began is not known, and this chapter looks for that beginning in the carpet belt of Uttar Pradesh, starting with the firm that made the Astana carpet.

## Hands, and a European's business of 1881

Hands says it descends from a carpet business that A. Tellery, a European, set up in 1881 to organise Bhadohi's weavers.[@co-hands] By the company's account, the business was sold after Independence to a Calcutta Marwari house, and the Patodia family took control around 1980 (another account says at the end of the Raj) and built the Hands brand.[@co-hands] The line from Tellery to Hands is the company's own; it has not been independently documented.[@co-hands; @ner-in-colonial-bhadohi]

<Figure id="co-hands" caption="Hands (Hands Carpets / Patodia Exports) of Bhadohi, which makes hand-knotted and hand-tufted carpets." />

Hands now makes hand-tufted carpets alongside hand-knotted ones.[@co-hands] Its story puts a European at the start of its own trade at Bhadohi, and the firm is not the only one in the belt with a European founder.[@ner-in-colonial-bhadohi]

## Obeetee's three initials, Mirzapur, 1920

Obeetee carries its founders in its name. It is formed from the initials of three Britons, F. H. Oakley, F. H. Bowden and J. A. L. Taylor, who set the firm up at Mirzapur in 1920; their partnership was dissolved in 1932 and replaced by a company named Obeetee.[@co-obeetee]

<Figure id="co-obeetee" caption="Obeetee, founded at Mirzapur in 1920 by F. H. Oakley, F. H. Bowden and J. A. L. Taylor." />

Obeetee too now makes hand-tufted rugs as well as hand-knotted ones, and describes the process: yarn is punched into canvas with a tufting gun and then glued to a backing.[@co-obeetee] When it began to tuft has not been found.[@co-obeetee] The founding years of the old firms are on record; the arrival of the gun is not.

## Surya at Ugapur, 1976

The exporters founded after Independence show the same mix. Surya began in 1976 at Ugapur, in the Bhadohi area, as a small supplier of hand-knotted rugs, after a meeting with a buyer from the Federated Group. It is now a large American supplier of hand-knotted, hand-tufted and flatwoven rugs.[@co-surya] Jaipur Living, founded in Rajasthan in 1978, is mainly a hand-knotting firm, but also makes rugs gun-tufted through a pre-woven cloth with a cotton-latex backing.[@co-jaipur] Kaleen India, begun in 1996, has plants in Panipat, Mirzapur and Bikaner making hand-knotted, hand-tufted, flatweave and power-loom rugs.[@co-kaleen-india] Surya and Jaipur Living, and their American offices, return in chapter 11.

Panipat, in Haryana, owes its carpet industry, by one account, to the older belt. A CUTS International case study says the industry spread there "where the weavers migrated from Bhadohi-Mirzapur".[@ner-in-panipat-migration] The town is now also a centre of textile recycling: *The Tribune* reports more than 30 lakh kilograms of recycled yarn a day, and about 200 spinning mills (150 in another version of the story).[@ner-in-panipat-recycling] Surya, Jaipur Living and Kaleen all tuft, and none of their records says when they started.

## A sentence in a 2019 journal article

No source yet found dates the arrival of hand-tufting in India.[@ner-in-date-gap] The research brief reports that a Shodhganga thesis, papers from IICT, the carpet-technology institute at Bhadohi, the Carpet Export Promotion Council's history, the National Handicrafts Policy report, the Harvard FXB Center's report *Tainted Carpets* and a CUTS International case study were all checked and are silent on the date. The thesis, by that account, mentions "tufted guns" in its Bhadohi chapter. Those checks have not been re-verified one by one.[@ner-in-date-gap]

The nearest thing to a date is one sentence, without a reference, in a 2019 article on India's carpet-weaving tradition by Pranshi Jain, an assistant professor of architecture and planning at MITS Gwalior. Jain lists tufted carpets among "contemporary" carpets.[@ner-in-jain]

<PullQuote from="ner-in-jain" who="Pranshi Jain, “Carpet Weaving Tradition of India”, 2019">In India, this technique came after the India achieved independence. This was the innovation technique which was introduced in this industry for making the production faster.</PullQuote>

When hand-tufting overtook hand-knotting is undated too. An earlier reading put the change between 1995 and 2005. It rested on a Hindi passage, since not found again, about the shrinking market share of हस्तनिर्मित carpet, read as hand-knotted; the word means handmade, which in the trade includes hand-tufted, so the passage says nothing about tufting against knotting.[@ner-in-crossover] *Amar Ujala*'s reports of 2025 and 2026 say only that knotted carpets are now a token share of exports and that hand-tufted and handloom goods dominate.[@ner-in-crossover] The written record gives no date, but the institute at Bhadohi shows how the technique is now passed on.

## The IICT syllabus, and guns for sale

IICT runs under the Ministry of Textiles. It hosts, as its old syllabus, the B.Tech course in carpet and textile technology issued by U.P. Technical University, which includes the "practice of tufting using hand & electric gun" and lists "tufting equipments used e.g. (tufting frame, tufting gun etc.) Schortz hand tufted machine".[@ner-in-iict-syllabus]

<Figure id="ner-chase-goswami" size="margin" caption="K. K. Goswami (ed.), Advances in Carpet Manufacture, second edition (Woodhead Publishing, 2017)." />

K. K. Goswami, named as IICT's director, edited *Advances in Carpet Manufacture* (second edition 2017), which has chapters on handmade carpets and a separate chapter on tufting. The research treats it as the most likely place for a sourced chronology of Indian hand-tufting; it is behind a paywall.[@ner-chase-goswami] His publisher's biography calls him the "inventor of a new form of carpet backing"; no Indian patent for a tufting gun has been found.[@ner-in-backing-patents]

The tools are sold locally. In Mirzapur, Naksh Enterprises sells new manual speed-tufting tools for cut and loop pile up to 45 mm, at around ₹1,500, though commercial pile work uses 220-volt electric guns.[@ttw-mirzapur-manual] The manual tool's history is told in chapter 9. At Meerut, Sheru Bros Engineering Works, a partnership firm that lists its founding as 1965, advertises electric hand-tufting guns, one at ₹9,000, and says it exports them to Egypt and South Africa; all of this is the seller's own listing.[@ner-in-sheru] What the trade calls these tools is the next clue.

## A word on Hindi Wikipedia

Hindi has a word for tufted carpet, गुच्छित कालीन (*guchchhit kālīn*), from गुच्छ, a tuft or cluster. It appears in the Hindi Wikipedia article on carpets, and a search for the exact phrase found it on no other Hindi page.[@ner-in-vocabulary] The trade writes English words in Devanagari instead: हैंड टफ्टेड (hand tufted) and टफ्टिंग गन (tufting gun), as in an *Amar Ujala* headline on हैंड टफ्टेड कालीनों.[@ner-in-vocabulary]

The same Wikipedia article was once recorded as saying that the machine for making carpet by stitching was invented in the southern United States shortly after the Second World War. The sentence did not appear when the page was searched again, and its old-fashioned phrasing suggests it was taken from a mid-century Hindi encyclopedia.[@ner-in-hindi-wiki] The handmade belt, with its borrowed words, is not the whole of Indian tufting, however.

## A plant near Hyderabad, 2016

<Figure id="co-welspun-flooring" caption="Welspun Flooring, formed in 2016 as a subsidiary of the home-textile maker Welspun India." />

Industrial carpet came to India separately from the handmade belt. Welspun Flooring was formed in March 2016 as a subsidiary of the home-textile maker Welspun India. Its plant near Hyderabad, under construction in 2018 for a start in 2019, makes carpet tiles, broadloom, artificial grass and click vinyl. The company calls it Asia's largest; when it was commissioned has not been confirmed.[@co-welspun-flooring]

Bhadohi's American business, meanwhile, has been exposed to tariffs. A 50 per cent American tariff took effect on 27 August 2025. *Amar Ujala* reported that 46 American buyers were expected at Bhadohi's October fair, against a usual 55 or so, and the fair had about 110 fewer stalls. The tariff was cut to 18 per cent in an India–US agreement in February 2026.[@ner-in-tariff-2025] The Astana carpet was reported in the same month as that shrunken fair.[@ner-in-guinness-2025; @ner-in-tariff-2025]

This chapter argues that hand-tufting entered an export belt some of whose best-known firms were founded by Europeans, that it left no dated record of its arrival, and that the trade still names it in borrowed English words. Its weight in the trade today is reported; its beginning remains to be found.
