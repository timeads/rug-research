On 20 October 1989 China issued a national standard, GB 11746-89, titled 簇绒地毯, "Tufted carpet". Its main drafter was the Tianjin Carpet Research Institute.[@ner-cn-gbt11746] Tianjin was an old carpet city; between 1912 and 1927, according to the *Tianjin Daily*, it shipped more than 80 per cent of China's carpet exports.[@ner-cn-tianjin] Tufted carpet was new. The earliest dated trace of it found so far in mainland China is from the 1980s.[@ner-cn-1970s; @ner-cn-gbt11746] This chapter follows China's carpets from the palace and the export workshop to the imported tufting line and a carpet township in Tianjin, to see how an old trade took up a new technique.

## Nearly a thousand carpets in the Palace Museum

The Palace Museum in Beijing holds nearly 1,000 Ming and Qing carpets. More than 300 of them, Xinhua reported in 2024, came from Ningxia, and most of those from Zhongwei; Ningxia's hand-made carpet weaving was listed as national intangible heritage in 2021.[@ner-cn-ningxia] (An earlier note in this research put the Ningxia share at over half, apparently a misreading of the Chinese for "most", which referred to Zhongwei's share.[@ner-cn-ningxia])

Beijing palace carpet weaving is also on the state register of intangible heritage. Its entry says that carpet weaving came into China from the Western Regions in the Western Han and was largely mature in the Central Plains by the Tang. It records a gold medal at the Paris Exposition of 1900, 354 workshops in Beijing in 1920, and the founding in 1956 of the joint state-private Beijing No. 1 Carpet Factory. It also warns, in translation: "no successors; the technique faces the danger of being lost".[@ner-cn-palace-carpet]

How the Beijing workshops began is disputed. Popular histories credit a lama who brought carpets to the capital for the Xianfeng emperor and set up production at Baoguo Temple near Guang'anmen, founding the "Eastern Gate" and "Western Gate" methods. A publication of the Beijing Municipal Cultural Heritage Bureau dates this to 1860 or 1871; another version moves it to the Qianlong reign, with three schools and teachers from Gansu; and the Beijing Carpet Research Institute, in its research of the 1960s and 1970s, found records only of tribute carpets and no documentary basis for the lama.[@ner-cn-tibet-1860] Whatever their origin, by the 1920s the workshops of Beijing and Tianjin were weaving for buyers abroad.

## A survey of Tianjin's carpet shops, 1929

In 1929 (some sources say 1930) the Nankai Institute of Economics surveyed Tianjin's carpet trade and counted 303 carpet factories and workshops, 2,749 looms and 11,568 workers.[@ner-cn-tianjin] From Nankai's Committee on Social and Economic Research came H. D. Fong's (方显廷) *Tientsin Carpet Industry* (《天津地毯工业》), dated 1929 or 1930, the first of his six-volume series of industry studies. It is reprinted in 《方显廷文存》 (2019), so it can still be had in print.[@ner-chase-tianjin-primary]

The trade it described was built for export. The *Tianjin Daily* gives the city 94.1 per cent of China's carpet exports in 1916 and 97.4 per cent in 1926.[@ner-cn-tianjin] Tianjin's own media are now recovering how that began. A Sohu documentary series, 「文献里的天津」, gives an episode to Wm. Pustau & Co. (鲁麟洋行), founded at Canton in 1845 by the Hamburg merchant Pustau, which opened a Tianjin branch in 1890. It was an order house, not a maker, and its title of "earliest exporter of Chinese carpets" rests on a story about one of its managers seeing carpets at the Yonghegong.[@ner-chase-sohu-series]

Beijing's trade boomed on exports in the 1920s too. A popular history names an American called Fette, who with the merchant Li Lixuan (李黎轩, also 李梦书) founded the Yanjing Carpet Factory (燕京地毯厂); by about 1924 it had six workshops, more than 1,200 workers and a quarter to a third of Beijing's output.[@ner-cn-beijing-export] The work was hard. A survey of 207 Beijing carpet shops in 1924 found 6,834 workers, 74 per cent of them apprentices, with 94 per cent working 12 to 14 hours a day and 93 per cent working seven days a week.[@ner-cn-beijing-export]

The research brief claimed that the collectors' categories "Nichols rugs" and "Chinese Art Deco" do not exist in Chinese. That was wrong. Tianjin histories name Nichols as 倪克, a carpet factory said to have been founded by an American in 1923, and pair it with Fette as the two largest American carpet houses, though mainstream Chinese histories sort the period's designs by their own types (美术式, 彩花式, 北京式, 素凸式) rather than by "Art Deco".[@ner-art-deco-not-chinese]

Tianjin's later century was mixed. Its "Sailing Ship" (风船) brand won gold medals at Leipzig in 1965 and 1986. From the 1990s, according to the *Tianjin Daily*, the city's carpets lost their advantage in export conditions and in competition at home, and the old plants, led by the No. 2 and No. 3 carpet factories, were gradually displaced by urban construction.[@ner-cn-tianjin] The new kind of carpet appears most clearly, in the records found so far, at a factory of the petrochemical industry.

## The Yanshan petrochemical carpet factory

A 2004 taxpayer profile in *China Taxation* describes the Beijing Yanshan Petrochemical Company Carpet Factory, predecessor of the Shuangquan Yanshan Carpet Company, as "China's earliest machine-woven carpet enterprise … the cradle of China's modernised carpet industry" (in translation). Those words are the company's own.[@ner-cn-yanshan] Chinese Wikipedia dates the factory's name, 燕化公司化纤地毯厂, to 1 November 1987.[@ner-cn-yanshan]

What the profile shows most clearly is where the machines came from. Its main equipment, it says, was imported: tufting lines from CMC and Tuftco in the United States and Cobble in Britain, Wilton looms from Belgium and Germany, and a line for bulked continuous filament yarn from Italy (on that yarn, see chapter 6).[@ner-cn-yanshan]

When the first tufting machine reached mainland China is less certain. A promotional post by a seller of printers says that machine-made carpet began large-scale production in China in the 1970s, which carries little weight. A 2013 trade article, seen once in a search summary and not found again, is more careful: China began its own research on machine-made carpet in the 1970s and imported its first tufting machine in 1980.[@ner-cn-1970s] The Yanshan firm also says it helped draft the national standards for woven and tufted carpet,[@ner-cn-yanshan] the documents to which the story now turns.

## Two standards, 1989 and 2011

The tufted-carpet standard of 1989 came into force on 1 July 1990. Its main drafter, the Tianjin Carpet Research Institute, belonged to the old export city, which thus took the lead in writing the rules for the new product. The standard was replaced by GB/T 11746-2008, in force from March 2009.[@ner-cn-gbt11746]

A second standard, GB/T 27729-2011, names the carpet made with the hand gun precisely: 手工枪刺胶背地毯, "hand gun-tufted glue-backed carpet".[@ner-cn-handmade-label] The trade has words of its own as well: 簇绒 for tufting in general, 簇绒机 for the industrial machine, 簇绒枪 for the gun, and 枪刺, "gun-stab", for hand-tufting.[@ner-cn-terminology]

Sellers shorten the standard's name. They drop the "glue-backed" and sell 手工枪刺地毯, so buyers hear 手工, "handmade". One source calls such carpets "mistakenly called handmade", though Baidu Baike counts them as handmade carpet; the main sources are sellers of hand-knotted carpet, with an interest in the distinction. The research brief sees the same slippage in "hand-tufted" in English.[@ner-cn-handmade-label] The first Chinese-built tufting machine found so far had been commissioned only seven years before.[@ner-cn-dht200]

## The DHT-200 tufting machine, 2004

In 2004 the Huayuan Group Carpet Company commissioned the DHT-200 tufting machine from the School of Mechanical Engineering at Donghua University, which said it "filled a gap in China's carpet-equipment industry" (in translation). The sources disagree on the client: Baidu Baike places the company in Shanghai, a 2007 Donghua item names Jiangxi Huayuan Group.[@ner-cn-dht200] In 2010 the Donghua team under Sun Yize (孙以泽) won a second-class National Science and Technology Progress Award for a series of tufted-carpet looms and their industrialisation.[@ner-cn-sun-yize] Programmed and robotic tufting in China is taken up in chapter 12.

The hand gun was being patented in China in the same year. A Chinese utility model filed in April 2004 covers an electric hand-held tufting gun, then called an "electric weaving needle" (电织针),[@pat-cn2723478y] and a second, filed that November, describes one with a small motor, a gear-transmission box and a connecting rod.[@pat-cn2806530y] The gun's longer history is told in chapter 9, which also follows Hong Kong's Tai Ping Carpets from hand-knotting to hand-tufting, by its own account.[@co-tai-ping] Tai Ping had already moved all its carpet production to Nanhai, in Guangdong, by July 1992.[@ner-hk-taiping-1992]

Imports did not stop. Chinese customs records for March 2025 include one tufted-carpet loom imported into Jiangsu at US$665,441 and one into Zhejiang at US$471,660; in February Jiangsu had imported 40 units under the same code for US$736,158 in all, so many imports under the code are of low value.[@ner-cn-imports-2025] Foreign makers also patent in China, as Card-Monroe did with a tufting-machine application of 2017, though Chinese tufting-machine patents exist too.[@ner-cn-patents]

## Cuihuangkou, a carpet township in Tianjin

Cuihuangkou (崔黄口镇) is a Tianjin township of under 100 square kilometres and about 50,000 people. Official and Xinhua figures give it more than 1,200 carpet firms, more than 100 million carpets a year, and about 40 per cent of China's carpet exports. Chinese state media group it with Dalton and Gaziantep as one of the world's three great carpet clusters, but as an epithet; no official designation has been found. A 2020 business feature said that "eight of every ten carpets in China come from Cuihuangkou" (in translation), and in the same piece that the town made half the country's carpets; neither figure has a statistical source.[@ner-cn-cuihuangkou]

Those carpets go to a world market that China now leads. China is the largest carpet exporter, with 23.5 per cent of world exports in 2023, according to ITC figures reported by the trade site Ebrun; its carpet exports that year were worth US$4.0 billion, according to the CHTA.[@ner-cn-exports-iraq] For carpets made in Tianjin, in the first ten months of what was probably 2024, the largest market was Iraq, with 19.6 per cent, ahead of the United States at 17.1 per cent and Malaysia at 11.2 per cent.[@ner-cn-exports-iraq]

One maker in the township describes what a newer process has done to output. Bai Hongwei of Kaili Carpet, speaking in about August 2024 after his firm moved to heat-transfer printing, said in translation: "Now we produce 500,000–600,000 m² a month; before, that took ten years."[@ner-cn-heat-transfer]

This chapter argues that China took up tufting late and through imported machines, from the 1980s at the earliest on present evidence, with the first Chinese-built tufting machine found so far commissioned in 2004, yet the old carpet city still shaped the new industry. Tianjin, which had carried the carpet export trade of the 1910s and 1920s, drafted the national standard for tufted carpet in 1989 and is now home to the township that ships about two-fifths of the country's carpet exports.[@ner-cn-tianjin; @ner-cn-1970s; @ner-cn-gbt11746; @ner-cn-dht200; @ner-cn-cuihuangkou]
