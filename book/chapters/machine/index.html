Shade 5Ochre1920–1981

Building the machine

The tufting machine began as an attachment for the sewing machine, patented in Tennessee and Georgia from the 1920s, and became the multi-needle broadloom machine of the Chattanooga builders.

By Tim Eads8 minute read47 notes, 51 sources, 1 checked at the archivePrototype draft

One machine of these years has its own monument. The carpet tufting apparatus of Ernest J. Moench, dated 1928 and now displayed at Shaw Industries in Dalton, was a single-needle machine that punched loops of thick yarn through a backing; Moench patented a multi-needle version, which was cited in improvement patents as late as 1980. The American Society of Mechanical Engineers named it Historic Mechanical Engineering Landmark no. 266 on 31 July 2018 and credits Moench with the first machine, where Dalton accounts credit Looper. Moench’s patent number has not been found, and ASME sources give both a “1928 patent” and one “issued 1934”.1313“Moench carpet tufting apparatus”, 1928, Shaw Industries. Record

The machine builders

The machines were built by a few firms, mostly around Chattanooga, and their histories are known so far mainly from their own accounts and from search summaries still to be checked. According to Card-Monroe’s history, the Cobble brothers began making tufting equipment for chenille bedspreads in the 1930s.3333“Cobble (Cobble Brothers / Singer-Cobble / Vandewiele)”, 1930s (Cobble brothers); UK subsidiary 1955, Cobble (Cobble Brothers / Singer-Cobble / Vandewiele). Record “Card-Monroe Corp. (CMC)”, founded 1981 (as Charles Monroe Company); Card-Monroe 1983, Card-Monroe Corp. (CMC). The same summaries say that Spencer H. Wright led Cobble for more than thirty years, with plants in Blackburn and Dalton, and Vandewiele’s own timeline says that the Belgian group bought the firm in 2013.3434“Cobble (Cobble Brothers / Singer-Cobble / Vandewiele)”, 1930s (Cobble brothers); UK subsidiary 1955, Cobble (Cobble Brothers / Singer-Cobble / Vandewiele). The Card family carried the trade on in Chattanooga. Tuftco says it was founded in 1960 as Southern Machine Company to build and sell tufting machines, and sold them in Europe, Asia and South America in the 1960s; an obituary of Lewis Card Sr. says instead that he and his brother Roy, after working at Cobble, founded Tuftco “in the late 1950s”.3939“Tuftco”, founded 1960 (as Southern Machine Company), Tuftco. Record Card-Monroe says that Roy and Lewis Card Sr., the Cobbles’ nephews, started at Cobble in the 1940s (an obituary says Lewis joined in 1939). The Charles Monroe Company was founded in 1981 by Charles Monroe and Lewis Card Jr., and became Card-Monroe in 1983 when Lewis and Roy Card invested. By the company’s own figures it has more than 2,500 machines in 38 countries.4040“Card-Monroe Corp. (CMC)”, founded 1981 (as Charles Monroe Company); Card-Monroe 1983, Card-Monroe Corp. (CMC). Many of the needles now come from Germany. According to the company’s history, as summarised in search results, Groz-Beckert of Ebingen made only knitting needles until 1980; in 1998 it bought the tufting-needle business of Josef Zimmermann of Aachen, sold under the brand “Eisbär”, and then added tufting knives, loopers and reed fingers by acquisition up to 2004. It is now a major supplier of needles and parts for tufting machines.4141“Groz-Beckert (tufting needles)”, founded 1852; tufting needles from 1998, Groz-Beckert (tufting needles). This chapter argues that the tufting machine began as an attachment for the sewing machine, patented in Tennessee and Georgia from 1920, more than a decade before Looper’s Dalton patent, and that once its needles were multiplied in the 1940s and the machine was widened to broadloom, its building settled with a few firms in Chattanooga and Blackburn.
