Chapter 131973–2021

The hand tool returns: hobby tufting and poke-poke

Home tufting tools had been sold for decades, but after 2018 the powered hand gun itself left the workshop for the home, and the Chinese press credits social media with carrying the craze to mainland China as “poke-poke”.

In 1930 a donor to the Auckland Museum picked up a rug-hooking tool in Eden Terrace, Auckland. It has wooden handles painted with flowers on black, a large needle in the centre, and a maker’s mark in gold on a handle: “Jewel Pat. Allowed”. The museum calls it a semi-automated tool for punching pile through backing, patented in the United States in 1886.11““Jewel” rug-hooking tool, patented 1886 (Auckland Museum 1932.212.1)”, by 1930 (patent 1886, per the museum's record), Auckland War Memorial Museum (via Wikimedia Commons), 1932.212.1. Record Hand tools for punching pile into a backing are much older than the hand-gun tufting hobby of the years after 2018. This chapter follows such tools through boxed kits to the powered gun, and to what changed when the gun came home.

Ye “Susan Burr”, in its box from Torrington

The “Susan Burr” was a boxed hand rug machine with printed instructions, of a type dated to the 1920s. The box front reads “Made by The Holley Associates, Torrington, Conn.”22“Ye ‘Susan Burr’ Hooked Rug Machine”, c. 1920s–1930s, Tuft the World (own collection). Record The tool has two wooden handles that slide against each other, a steel needle in one and a gauge in the other. The instructions tell the user to “push down the gauge to hold the loop while the needle is withdrawn”, alternately inserting the needle and the gauge.33“Ye ‘Susan Burr’ Hooked Rug Machine”, c. 1920s–1930s, Tuft the World (own collection). Record The research notes on the box find that its mechanism copies Ebenezer Ross’s patent of 1891 (chapter 9), and that no record of a real Susan Burr exists.44“Ye ‘Susan Burr’ Hooked Rug Machine”, c. 1920s–1930s, Tuft the World (own collection). Record This box, and the Ronco kit below, are in the collection of Tuft the World, the firm co-founded by this book’s author.55“Ye ‘Susan Burr’ Hooked Rug Machine”, c. 1920s–1930s, Tuft the World (own collection). Record “Ronco ‘Speed-Tufting Kit’ (#1500), 1975”, © 1975, Tuft the World (own collection), Ronco #1500. Record

The box gave the tool a past. A poem on it ends:66“Ye ‘Susan Burr’ Hooked Rug Machine”, c. 1920s–1930s, Tuft the World (own collection). Record

The same two-handed action, needle and then holder in turn, was still on sale about half a century later.

Ronco’s Speed-Tufting Kit, 1975

In 1975 Ronco sold a “Speed-Tufting Kit” on television for $9.95. The box end says it was distributed exclusively by Ronco Teleproducts of Chicago, and the burlap backing inside is printed with an owl and ”© 1975 Southbury Mfg. Corp.”, the Connecticut maker.77“Ronco ‘Speed-Tufting Kit’ (#1500), 1975”, © 1975, Tuft the World (own collection), Ronco #1500. Record The tool is moulded plastic, with a needle handle and a looper handle worked in turn. The booklet explains: “With the Looper all the way up punch the needle through the backing material to form the first loop. Then push the Looper down to hold down the formed loop.”88“Ronco ‘Speed-Tufting Kit’ (#1500), 1975”, © 1975, Tuft the World (own collection), Ronco #1500. Record

The kit was built for home use from end to end. A fabric shredder cut old clothes, sheets or blankets into strips to tuft with, and an order sheet sold frames, latex and patterns by mail.99“Ronco ‘Speed-Tufting Kit’ (#1500), 1975”, © 1975, Tuft the World (own collection), Ronco #1500. Record The patent very likely behind it, filed by John S. Doyel in October 1975, was titled “Rug-tufting device suitable for home use by hobyists”.1010John S. Doyel, “Rug-tufting device suitable for home use by hobyists” (US3968757A), filed 31 Oct 1975, granted 13 July 1976. Record “Ronco ‘Speed-Tufting Kit’ (#1500), 1975”, © 1975, Tuft the World (own collection), Ronco #1500. Record

Tools with the same two-part alternating action are still sold new in Mirzapur, where Naksh Enterprises offers one for cut and loop pile up to 45 mm, at around ₹1,500 (chapter 10).1111“Manual speed-tufting tools are still sold new in Mirzapur, India”, research note. Record All of these were manual tools. The powered gun has a separate history, in workshops and mills.

The ZQ-III, from Shantou

The ZQ-III is a pneumatic tufting gun from Dayang, a carpet-machinery maker in Shantou, Guangdong. Dayang’s website, seen so far only in search excerpts, calls it “a new portable type tufting machine” that combines an “electric & pneumatic device”, and lists an electric tufting gun among its large tufting machines.1212“Dayang (Shantou, China): pneumatic and electric tufting guns, including the ZQ-III”, current (2026). Record

Guns like it have a history told in chapter 9. According to the Hong Kong Heritage Project, Tai Ping fitted the tufting gun with a motor and reduced its weight in 1973, though other sources give other dates.1313“1973: Tai Ping motorises the tufting gun”, 1973, research note. Record The records cited here find it in workshops and mills. Kramis in Switzerland makes bespoke rugs with single-needle tufting guns, and Shaw’s design team told Eads in conversation that hand guns still mend broadloom at final inspection.1414“Kramis Teppich Design”, founded 1987, Kramis Teppich Design. Record “Hand tufting guns are still used to mend broadloom at final inspection”, 2026 (conversation), Shaw Industries (design team, in conversation). Record The German firm Hofmann, whose beginnings chapter 9 follows, still lists a pneumatic hand-tufting gun for cut and loop pile, the VML 16, according to its trade-fair listing as seen in search results.1515“Hofmann VML 16: pneumatic hand-tufting gun for cut and loop pile (Germany)”, current (2026); spec sheet marked V2022 (per search excerpt). Record In Meerut, Sheru Bros Engineering Works, established in 1965 according to its own listing (another directory says fourteen years), advertises electric hand tufting guns on IndiaMART, one at about ₹9,000, and says it exports them to Egypt and South Africa. Every one of those details is the seller’s own.1616“India now exports tufting guns: Sheru Brothers, Meerut (est. 1965)”. Record

Tuft the World, the author’s firm, created a store record for the ZQ-III in May 2018, though that is not a confirmed first-sale date. Its listing says the gun is “used in factory settings” and does both cut and loop pile.1717“High-pile pneumatic tufting guns sold by Tuft the World: ZQ-III and AK-III”, ZQ-III product record created May 2018 (now archived); AK-III on sale Oct 2026. Record By the author’s own account, Tuft the World’s ZQ guns were made by Dayang.1818“Tuft the World’s ZQ guns were made by Dayang; its other guns are supplied under its own name”, stated 28 Oct 2026, research note. Record

Electric guns from a Philadelphia store, 2018–2024

Tuft the World, the author’s firm, was founded in 2018 by Tiernan Alexander and Tim Eads, artists who live and work in West Philadelphia. It sells tufting tools and materials and runs workshops.1919“Tuft the World”, founded 2018, Tuft the World. Record Its electric guns, most of them about 3 lb, are sold under its own name, with models for cut pile, for loop pile and for both, and several of its store pages address beginners as well as advanced tufters. Its own store records, which are not confirmed first-sale dates, put the first two in 2018.2020“Tuft the World’s electric tufting guns: AK-I, AK-II, KRD-I, the Duo and AK5 (2018–2026)”, product records created 2018–2024; the Duo and AK5 on sale Oct 2026. Record By the author’s own account, the guns other than the ZQ series are supplied under Tuft the World’s name.2121“Tuft the World’s ZQ guns were made by Dayang; its other guns are supplied under its own name”, stated 28 Oct 2026, research note. Record

Instruction went with the tools. In March 2024 the firm gave a lecture and workshop at The Fabric Workshop and Museum,2222“Tuft the World”, founded 2018, Tuft the World. Record and the next month Chronicle Books published the founders’ illustrated manual for tufting with a hand gun, Tuft the World: An Illustrated Manual to Tufting Gorgeous Rugs, Decor, and More.2323Tiernan Alexander, Tuft the World: An Illustrated Manual to Tufting Gorgeous Rugs, Decor, and More, 2024 (published 16 April 2024). Record How many people took up the hobby is not yet known: no sales or user figures have been found. Its spread is easier to see in the Chinese press.

The Southern Metropolis Daily, 14 November 2022

On 14 November 2022 the Southern Metropolis Daily (南方都市报) ran a story headed “帆板、露营之后，年轻人又开始戳戳秀了”: after paddleboarding and camping, young people turn to poke-poke.2424“The tufting gun came back to mainland China as a TikTok trend: 戳戳秀, ‘poke-poke’”, c. 2021, research note. Record 戳戳秀, also written 戳戳绣, “poke-poke”, was the name under which tufting with a hand gun became a craze in mainland China.2525“The tufting gun came back to mainland China as a TikTok trend: 戳戳秀, ‘poke-poke’”, c. 2021, research note. Record

According to the 2022 press, tufting studios opened first in Hangzhou, then in Guangzhou, where one shop opened in September 2021, and by early 2022 in Hefei and Jinan. The press, so far read only in search-result summaries, credits overseas social media (Instagram, also TikTok and Xiaohongshu) and the artist Tim Eads, with the year 2018, as sparks of the trend.2626“The tufting gun came back to mainland China as a TikTok trend: 戳戳秀, ‘poke-poke’”, c. 2021, research note. Record Eads is the author of this book, and the attribution is the press’s, not a measured influence.2727“Tuft the World”, founded 2018, Tuft the World. Record

Social media carried studios as well as hobbyists. An Instagram tufting video in 2022 brought the Swiss studio Kramis, founded in 1987, international attention (chapter 11).2828“Kramis Teppich Design”, founded 1987, Kramis Teppich Design. Record While the gun was going into homes, it was also being fitted back onto machines.

A gun for a carpet robot, Shandong, 2020

In June 2020 the Yangxin Ruixin Group, a Shandong carpet company, filed for a “pneumatic multifunctional carpet tufting gun for an intelligent gun-tufted carpet robot”, published that September.2929Yangxin Ruixin Group, “Pneumatic multifunctional carpet tufting gun for an intelligent gun-tufted carpet robot” (CN111705448A), filed 30 Jun 2020; published 25 Sep 2020. Record The gun is meant to be the working end of a robot.3030Yangxin Ruixin Group, “Pneumatic multifunctional carpet tufting gun for an intelligent gun-tufted carpet robot” (CN111705448A), filed 30 Jun 2020; published 25 Sep 2020. Record It is one of a cluster of Chinese filings for automated gun-tufting: a yarn-feeding and tensioning device for a gun-tufting machine, filed in January 2019, and a “fully automatic carpet gun-tufting machine head”, published in November 2020, both known so far only from search summaries.3131“Stable yarn-feeding and tensioning equipment for a carpet gun-tufting machine” (CN111441143A), filed 17 Jan 2019 (per search summary). Record “Fully automatic carpet gun-tufting machine head and its working method” (CN111876919A), priority 11 Aug 2020; published 3 Nov 2020. Record

In 2023 Tuft the World showed editioned rugs made on a large robotic tufting machine at Paradigm Gallery in Philadelphia.3232“TTW Editions at Paradigm Gallery: rugs made by a large robotic tufting machine (2023)”, 2023, Paradigm Gallery + Studio. Record Chapter 12 follows the machines.

This chapter argues that home tufting did not begin with the gun. Hand tools worked by needle and holder in turn were sold boxed in the 1920s and on television, for home use, in 1975. What changed after 2018 was that the powered hand gun, which the records cited here place in workshops and mills, became a home pastime and, carried by social media according to the Chinese press, reached mainland China as poke-poke by 2021, while in the same years the gun was being mounted on machines again.
