Research notes

How they work: the symmetric knot passes round two neighbouring warps and brings both ends up between them. The asymmetric knot wraps fully round one warp and only partly round the next, with one end coming up on each side, and can open to the left or right. Sources say the asymmetric knot allows finer, more curving designs, and the symmetric knot is very secure (Wikipedia; woven.is; Nazmiyal). Naming caveat: the geographic names are misleading. Wikipedia notes that symmetric knots are tied in the town of Senneh itself, and Iranian and non-Iranian weavers use both knots. Earliest evidence: Wikipedia says the Ghiordes (symmetric) knot is the one found in the Pazyryk fragments. For single-warp and Tibetan knots see process-knotted-spanish-knot and process-knotted-tibetan.
