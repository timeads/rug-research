Research notes

Polish-born brothers Stanislav (an artist, trained at the Pennsylvania Academy) and Thadius (an engineer) V'Soske set up a carpet workshop in Grand Rapids, Michigan; a local-history account dates the factory to 1920. Trade sources say Stanislav developed "the modern hand-tufting technique" in 1924; treat that as an attributed claim, since hand-tufting tools were being patented in the US in the same period. In 1941 MoMA commissioned V'Soske to make rugs from designs by American artists (Stuart Davis's "Flying Carpet" and Arshile Gorky's "Bull in the Sun" are in MoMA's collection). From 1979 to 1993 V'Soske commissioned 23 architects to design 38 rugs. In 1957 Vesta V'Soske set up a European base in Galway, Ireland, as V'Soske Joyce; one listing says that factory became the Dixon Carpet Company after Michael Dixon bought it in 1979. Stanislav died in 1983. The V&A holds 18 V'Soske objects (mostly 1970 samples). Scott Group's PWV Studios (hand-tufted, 1983) descends from V'Soske (see co-scott-group). Why it matters: the founding American hand-tufted studio and the source of the US art-rug tradition; the Irish venture is an early export of the technique to Europe. Dates: workshop 1920 (Oughterard Heritage) vs "mid-1920s" (dealer listings); 1924 "invention" is a trade-press claim, not established fact. Irish factory location (Moycullen vs Oughterard) and partners' names (Jimmy Joyce/Bart Monaghan vs James Joyce/Burke Monaghan) conflict. A Puerto Rico factory is sometimes mentioned but no source was found. No official company history page found.
