Research notes

How it works: a loose web (batt) of fibres passes under a beam of barbed felting needles. As they stab in and out, the barbs drag fibres through the web and tangle them into a felt-like fabric with no yarn. 'Structuring' looms with fork needles push fibre into loops or ribs to make pile carpets and mats (Duckett). History (one educational source): developed commercially in the late 1800s by William Bywater Ltd in England for jute, sisal and hair padding. Early looms ran about 100 strokes a minute and modern ones up to 2,000. Needled synthetic products entered home furnishings in the late 1950s, and random-velour looms arrived in the mid-1980s. The repo already notes that Toli (Japan) began selling needlepunch carpet in June 1967. Heuga's first carpet tile was needlefelt (see process-industrial-carpet-tile). Indoor-outdoor needlepunch claims were not confirmed.
