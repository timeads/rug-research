Research notes

How hand-knotting works: on a warped loom, the weaver ties short lengths of yarn around warp pairs, one row at a time. Each row is cut to height and locked with one or more ground wefts, and the knot ends form the pile (Wikipedia, 'Knotted-pile carpet'). What it shows: the earliest firm evidence of knotted pile. It is all wool with symmetric (Turkish/Ghiordes) knots. The field has a cross-like motif and the borders have deer and mounted horsemen. It is about 1.83 × 1.98 m (Schürmann 1982, from a Hermitage viewing). It was found in 1949 in Barrow 5, frozen in ice after looters left the tomb open. Conflicts: (1) Date: radiocarbon gives 5th–4th century BCE or 2,400–2,500 years old (Guinness; Wikipedia). The Hermitage listing dates Barrow 5 to 252–238 BCE, and other accounts put the main barrows in the 4th–3rd century BCE. A 2019 Hali article suggests even earlier pile weaving may exist. (2) Knot density varies by source: about 3,600 per dm² or 360,000 per m² (232 per in²), 250 per in², 278 per in². (3) Origin: Achaemenid Persian, 'Armenia or Persia' (Guinness), or unknown.
