Research notes

Hand-knotted context. Moore shared the 1757 Society of Arts premium (see rsa-carpet-premiums-1757-59). The Soane Museum says the Osterley drawing-room carpet was 'executed in accordance with Adam's design by Thomas Moore of Moorfields' and survives in place; the National Trust calls it a 'hand tufted pile carpet' (its wording). Christie's: Moore made silk stockings before knotted carpets, his success owed much to Adam, and his and Whitty's were the only two workshops to survive past the mid-century. Germain Havard came from Moore's works to Scotland in 1759 (Habib). The Met's Lansdowne-room carpet shares an Adam design with V&A and Philadelphia examples, attributed to 'Axminster or Moorfields'.
