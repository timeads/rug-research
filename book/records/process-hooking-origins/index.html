Research notes

How it works: strips of fabric or yarn are pulled up through a woven foundation (usually burlap) with a hook, making loops on the surface (Halifax Public Libraries; Canadian Museum of History). Sources seen did not describe the hand motion in more detail. Origin theories: (1) Kent's The Hooked Rug (1930, Dodd, Mead; Tudor reissues 1937/1941) traced it to early-1800s Yorkshire floor mats, with mill workers hooking thrum ends through a backing. This is reported second-hand by Wikipedia. (2) The Canadian Encyclopedia says it probably arose independently and at the same time in Québec, the Maritimes and New England. (3) A Colonial Williamsburg label says hooking seems to have begun in New England and south-east Canada in the 1840s. (4) Viking or Scandinavian origins (dealers, Jamar) are unsupported. Burlap: the Canadian Museum of History says burlap was not sold commercially in North America until the 1850s, just when hooking spread. Colonial Williamsburg says a lack of foundation cloth held the craft back until jute burlap arrived. The oldest known Canadian hooked rug was designed by Susanna Smith (16) and hooked by her sister Abigail (13) in 1860 at New Maryland near Fredericton, New Brunswick (CMH). Repo already has Winterthur hooked rugs and the Ross 1886 punch hook.
