Research notes

Two spreads with the peacock, the motif that gave U.S. Highway 41 its nickname "Peacock Alley". The yellow spread is a fan-tailed peacock on a branch, worked almost entirely in rows of separate cut tufts ("dots") in blue, red, pink, green, brown and white, with a scalloped tufted edge. The white spread has a standing pink peacock with blue "eyes" in a dense tufted tail, framed by green and pink foliage. Dense areas (the bodies) are packed rows of tufts; outlines are single rows. To record on a return visit (ask the center): accession numbers, maker or label, whether hand- or machine-tufted (look at the back: even stitch length and straight rows suggest a single-needle machine), and dates.
