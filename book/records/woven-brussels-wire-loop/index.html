Research notes

Britannica: a machine-made carpet with uncut loops, 'thought to have originated in or near Brussels', fashionable in the first half of the 19th century. The 1842 Encyclopaedia Britannica: Brussels and Wilton are woven 'in looms of the same construction' and named after the places where they were invented; long steel wires are inserted between linen and worsted warps, looping up the worsted pile. The NPS adds that Brussels was woven in strips on a 27-inch loom. Why it matters: this is the step from knotting pile by hand to forming pile mechanically on a loom, the technique that power-loom Brussels and Wilton later scaled up and that tufting eventually replaced. Gap: no source found ties the technique to Tournai specifically, except the Kidderminster legend that John Broom fetched it from 'Brussels, Tournai, or Wilton'. Dictionary.com dates the term 'Brussels carpet' to 1790–1800.
