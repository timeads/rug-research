Research notes

How it works: in soumak, coloured supplementary wefts are wrapped around the warps rather than woven over and under them. Typically each wrap goes forward over four warps and back under two, with plain ground wefts between the wrapping rows. The front looks like herringbone or embroidery, the back is left with loose ends, and there are no slits where colours meet (Wikipedia 'Soumak'). The name may come from Shemakha in Azerbaijan, but its origin is uncertain. This object: catalogued as plain weave with supplementary-weft wrapping, Caucasus, 1875–1925. Comparison: the Fitzwilliam Museum's T.3-1961 (Caucasian, 19th century, soumak with herringbone effect).
