Research notes

Correction to a common assumption: the NPS describes William Peter Sprague's Philadelphia Carpet Manufactory (1791) as the first full-fledged commercial carpet factory in America, but it used the Axminster hand-knotting technique (he may have trained under Thomas Whitty), not ingrain. Connecticut Historical Society material calls him William R. Sprague. Flat-woven ingrain arrives at mill scale later: the Tariff Manufacturing Company, Simsbury, incorporated 1825, was Connecticut's first carpet factory and made ingrain (the Winterthur dye notebooks in record wint-lib-tariff-brussels show it later dyed Brussels yarn). Orrin Thompson founded the Thompsonville works in 1828 (mill finished early 1829), making 'inexpensive flat-woven ingrain carpeting'; this became Bigelow-Hartford. A LeRoy Historical Society article reports ingrain was still over 50 percent of US production in 1899 (single source).
