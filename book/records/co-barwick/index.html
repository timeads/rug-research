Research notes

After Coast Guard service, Eugene Barwick sold tufted goods for Sears and in 1949 formed Barwick Mills. One local history says sales doubled in 1950 and passed $26 million by 1954; by 1971 it ran large plants in LaFayette and Dalton; it closed by the mid-1980s. The book "Carpet Capital" uses it as a key case of the tufted-carpet boom; the Georgia Encyclopedia names it among the "maverick" newcomers who used tufting machines for room-size carpet around 1950. Why it matters: the archetypal 1950s start-up that turned tufting into a broadloom carpet business, rather than a bedspread one. Dates: 1949 rests mainly on one local-history site; check against "Carpet Capital". The defunct firm has no official site.
