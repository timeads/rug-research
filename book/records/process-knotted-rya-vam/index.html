Research notes

How it works: rya/ryijy is knotted pile with long, widely spaced rows of knots and many ground picks between them, giving a shaggy, heavy fabric first used as a bedcover. This object: woollen pile knotted on linen warps, marked with the initials AFBD and the date 1814. Given by Roger Fry, and the 1924 file mentions a moth-eaten patch. The V&A also has a ryijy dated 1799 (T.8-1914). History: the Finnish intangible-heritage wiki puts the earliest certain reference in the mid-15th century, tied to a castellan of Häme Castle, and says early ryijys were bedcovers made by professional weavers at castles and Crown manors. The Hämeenlinna museum cites a 1547 Häme Castle inventory listing 55 rugs as the earliest record of colourful ryijys. Bukowskis says the oldest surviving Finnish ryijy is 17th-century. The claim of a 9th-century (Viking) origin (Wikipedia, dealers) has no dated evidence.
