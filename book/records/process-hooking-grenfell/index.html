Research notes

How it worked: the Industrial Department gave women kits of pre-dyed material and burlap with stencilled designs (dog teams, geese, polar bears), and they hooked the mats at home. A 1928 mission appeal asked supporters for worn stockings, so many mats are hooked with fine silk or rayon strips. The mission sold them through churches and supporters to fund medical work (Textile Museum of Canada; Canadian Encyclopedia; Meer). Conflicts: start date 1906 (Meer), 1909 (Paula Laverty, Silk Stocking Mats, McGill-Queen's, 2005, as summarised) or 1913 (Canadian Encyclopedia). Mats were made until the late 1960s, and output peaked in the late 1920s and early 1930s.
