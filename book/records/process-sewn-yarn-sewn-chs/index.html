Research notes

How it works: yarn is sewn with a needle in a continuous running stitch through a closely woven cloth, leaving loops on the face that are usually clipped (Getty AAT definition, via search). The Met's bed rugs use the same needle-worked running stitch, 'not hooked, as was once assumed'. This links yarn-sewn rugs to the bed rugs (1720s–1830s) already in the repo. This object: cut and uncut loops of multi-ply wool (or bunches of fine singles) on plain-woven linen. It has an oval medallion with a boy and girl under a grapevine and roses in the corners, 33 1/8 × 61 1/4 in. A family tradition links it to the Samuel Hanmer house (built 1765). Caveat: Colonial Williamsburg's lab found most grounds in its early sewn-rug exhibition were cotton, not linen, so check each fibre ID.
