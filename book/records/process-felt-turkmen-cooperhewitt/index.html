Research notes

How it works (as the museum describes it): dyed wool is laid out in a pattern on a reed mat, wetted and rolled, and the roll is kicked back and forth. It is then unrolled and finished by fulling on the forearms. The pattern is felted in, not sewn on. This object: a 251.5 × 144.8 cm felt with horn motifs (a protective symbol), made at home for family use. Comparison: the British Museum has a double-sided Yomut felt from 1960–70 with ram's-horn motifs (As1973,09.66). The Mary Museum in Turkmenistan names traditional felt patterns and says felt is rolled in reeds with hot water.
