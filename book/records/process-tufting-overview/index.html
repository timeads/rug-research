Research notes

How it works: unlike weaving, where pile and backing are made together, tufting adds pile yarns to a backing that already exists (Britannica Student, 'rug and carpet', which names weaving and tufting as today's main methods). A needle, or a bar of hundreds of needles, pierces the primary backing and leaves loops, which can be cut. The tufts are then locked with latex or another adhesive, often laminated to a secondary backing (Playrite technical sheet; US patent 3856596). Lineage already in this database: needle-sewn bed rugs and candlewick, Dalton chenille bedspreads, multi-needle machines, nylon broadloom and the hand-held tufting gun. One source says tufted products are now more than 90 percent of carpet (Duckett, needle-punched nonwovens).
