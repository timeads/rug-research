Research notes

How it works: cut nylon fibres are charged in an electric field so they fly end-first into a wet adhesive layer and stand upright. After curing, loose fibres are drawn off. The result is a dense 'pile' (Forbo cites about 80 million polyamide 6.6 fibres per m²) over a waterproof adhesive and glass-fibre layers. BD Online says flocked flooring is neither carpet nor resilient flooring, though it shares traits of both. History: BD Online and Forbo say the first electrostatically flocked floor covering was made by Flotex at Château-Renault in the 1960s. Low & Bonar took UK rights in 1971, launched at Interbuild in November 1971, and opened a factory at Codnor Gate, Ripley, in 1972. Conflict: a 1994 Spanish article calls Flotex British.
